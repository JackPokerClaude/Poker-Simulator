## 1. Before any chart: think first
Marc's six questions, every hand:
1. What's the villain's range?
2. What's our edge on him?
3. What's the effective stack?
4. What size did he use?
5. What's the rake?
6. How many players are left to act?

- **Charts tell you what, not why.** Against someone who never 3-bets, don't balance. Playing balanced against him is like randomizing rock-paper-scissors against a guy who only throws rock. Just throw paper.
- **Read the player, not the seat.** Live players often open the same range UTG as on the BTN; a lot of them "don't even know how to spell position." How wide they open matters more than where they sit. Seat only matters against pros using solved charts.

## 2. When to open wider or tighter
| Open WIDER when | Open TIGHTER when |
|---|---|
| You have a bigger postflop edge (fish in the blinds) | You have a smaller edge (a table of pros, regs in the blinds) |
| The table is passive | The table is aggressive (you get 3-bet and can't realize equity) |
| You're deeper | You're shallower |
| The rake is lower, or it's a time game | The rake is high (you need hands that win more) |
| You're in late position | You're in early position (more players can wake up) |
| You have fold equity postflop (fit-or-fold players when deep) | The table is full of calling stations (fewer bluffs, so you must out-hand them) |

**Deep vs shallow:** deeper, favor suited hands that make the nuts. At 100bb or less, favor offsuit broadways that make strong top pair (add KQo/AJo at EP, cut suited connectors).
- **[2025-02-18 HHP] the same shift, with sizes:** opens and 3-bets are BIGGER at 200bb and smaller at 100bb (an open of 3x instead of 4x), because live players over-defend up to 4x-5x at 200bb. Why the hands shift: shallow, top pair is worth all the money; deep, top pair isn't enough, so you want straights and flushes. Depth by street: `playbook-deep-stacks.md` and postflop sections 2, 5 and 6.
- **[2024-12-31 HHP] the pool 3-bets far more than it did in 2017** (from the SB with 88/99/AJs/AQo, and even T8s from the HJ vs a 66 open; in 2017 it was QQ/JJ+ and maybe AK). So open a little tighter from early position, since a hand is more likely to get 3-bet now. This is the same "aggressive table, open tighter" idea as the table above.

Not when the SB is aggressive or the BB is a short stack.

## 4. Limpers
- **Never open-limp** unless it's a special game (a bounty or a 7-2 game). Play raise or fold.
  - **A bounty example [2025-12-16]:** winning 3 pots in a row makes everyone pay the winner $100-200. The CO has two bounty buttons and UTG limps. Marc over-limps KT, because the bounty player will iso any two, KT is ahead of that iso range, and slightly stronger hands would limp-raise. Then he calls the raise. (An over-limp in a special game, not an open-limp.)
- **Vs an EP limper:** iso with a tight range and a big size [MP ISO vs EP LIMP]. EP limpers are stronger, because people "don't limp to limp-fold."
- **Vs a late limper:** iso much wider, but still tighter than your BTN open [BTN ISO vs CO LIMP]. Late limpers just want a cheap flop.
- **Overlimping:** only in soft, low-rake or time games. In high-rake or drop games, play raise or fold. *(The 2024 advice to "cut most limping" is superseded.)*
- **Iso size:** 6x + 1bb per limper in position, about 7x + 1bb per limper out of position. Size down under 50bb. Example: at 2/5 with one EP limper, iso to about $35.
- **From the straddle, when the BB limps or completes: iso any two [09-15].** Limpers raise or limp-raise their strong hands, so a BB complete is unbalanced and weak.
- **Vs a limper who calls ANY two preflop: iso LINEAR, not trash [04-14].** Vs most limpers, raising all trash is profitable because limpers bifurcate (they raise good hands and limp-raise traps). Vs a player who calls anything, use hands that out-kick him: A9o, K8s, K7s. *(Same logic as 09-15, with a different villain; both stand.)*

## 6. BB defense
- **Vs a passive opener, about 7.5%** [BB vs PASSIVE OPEN]: defend relatively tight. 3-bet only very good hands: "jacks or queens plus", KQs+, AKo.

## 9. Cold 4-betting (A opens, B 3-bets, you're next)
- **4-bet or fold.** Cold-calling a 3-bet turns your hand face up, and the opener can squeeze you out. Base your range on the 3-bettor, not the opener.
- **Vs a tight 3-bettor** [COLD 4B vs TIGHT 3B]: QQ+ and AKs, with JJ borderline.
- **Vs a wide 3-bettor** [COLD 4B vs WIDE 3B]: TT+, maybe AQo, AJs and KJs, with A5s mixed. Still linear.
- **Reading HIS cold 4-bet [06-16]:** once you've seen him cold-call your 3-bet with JJ, his cold-call range is TT-QQ, 99, AQs, KQs and AK. So his cold 4-bet is AA/KK (sometimes AK), and QQ is a "very, very easy fold." (Matches red flag 3.) **[2025-04-22 HHP]** the same read with QQ shown: his cold-call range includes QQ (also 66/77, T9s, AQs, and NOT necessarily capped), so his cold 4-bet is "pretty much only" KK/AA. After he cold 4-bets, "massively overfold" QQ and worse. You can't say his cold-call range is only strong hands.

## 10. Facing a 4-bet
- A rec's 4-bet is mostly AA or KK.
- **Your rule: 5-bet AA only** (it's in the CSV).
- Vs thinking or competent players at 200bb, he has no 5-bet range; he just calls the 4-bet.
  - Marc flats his whole continuing range vs good pros, even AA, KK and AKs [06-09, 06-30].
  - Vs fish, 5-bet AA: their 4-bet is strong and they won't fold KK/QQ/AKs [06-09].
  - "If you're not playing 5/10 or bigger, just 5-bet aces and only aces" [06-30].
- Vs a passive 4-bettor, QQ, JJ and TT are "right on the edge."
- No hand is comfortable getting it in for value 200bb deep against AA.
- **KK at 100bb vs an open-then-4-bet-jam: call [09-15].** Even nittier players jam AK, QQ and JJ at 100bb to avoid playing postflop. Fold KK only when you're sure he's a nit, you're 200bb+ deep, and he cold 4-bets or 5-bets.
- **3-bet sizing hints [09-15]:**
  - A habitual limper who suddenly opens to 15: 3-bet KK to 75-80, not 55. He has it and won't fold.
  - Vs a big 7x open (to 20): 3-bet to 90-100.

## 13. Red flags: when a rec is telling you he has it
Live players run on fear and greed, and they telegraph strength.
1. **A rec 5-bets at 200bb+:** it's AA. Consider folding KK; QQ is an auto-muck.
2. **A rec 4-bets to more than 35% of his stack without jamming:** heavily AA. He wants a call. *(2024 said one-third; superseded.)*
3. **A rec cold 4-bets:** under-bluffed. Recs cold-call with JJ, AK, QQ and TT.
   - [02-10] example: a new table, no hands seen. You open JJ in the CO, the BTN 3-bets, and an older man in the SB with coffee in the cup holder cold 4-bets. Close in theory, but vs him "it is not a close decision." (He never says the action out loud. The context implies a fold.)
   - [04-14]: a short-stacker who cold 4-bets half her stack has AA/KK (section 7).
4. **An unusually large open** (30-35 when normal is 20-25): a stronger range. The "standard" 3-bet vs a $30 open is $90-100; with AA, go egregiously large (2024: "five or six X" instead of the usual 3-4x). **[2025-12-16]** a rec's 9x UTG open (45 at 2/5) is "very telling," and betting into two players after it is "a pretty damn good hand" (AA, KK, AQ, some QQ). Marc's BB T6cc vs that open: always fold at 100-200bb (the CSV agrees, T6s folds in [BB vs PASSIVE OPEN] at 200bb), a speculative call only at 400bb (`playbook-deep-stacks.md` section 6).
5. **A habitual EP limper suddenly raises:** top 5-7%.
6. **A limp-reraise:** AA, KK, AKs, maybe QQ, with no bluffs. Overfold massively.
   - **The flip side [06-16]:** once a known limp-reraiser has shown AA, iso his limps with fewer hands and a smaller size. When he open-raises instead, his open is "crippled" (no AA/KK), so "three-bet with impunity."
   - This is a limp-RERAISER. Red flag 5's habitual limper who suddenly raises is "not a limp-raiser," so both stand.
7. **A BB 3-bet vs an EP open:** massively under-bluffed. Overfold.

## 14. Villain reads: what his range is, and how he reacts to our raises
All of these are Marc's descriptions, except the one tagged [04-28]. Nothing is inferred.
- **Passive rec 3-bettor:** JJ+ and AK. He won't fold QQ to a 4-bet, so with AA "size the hell up" on the 4-bet.
- **Aggressive thinking player:** 3-bets a lot and "can three bet fold occasionally." He's the only one worth 4-bet bluffing.
- **Aggressive whale:** "massively underfolding" to 4-bets. Stay linear, and size up your nutted hands.
- **Rec 4-bettor:** "often aces and kings, maybe some ace king." Fish and recs don't fold KK, AK or QQ to a 5-bet. That's why your 5-bet is a shove with AA only.
- **Rec cold-caller:** cold-calls a 3-bet with JJ, AK, QQ and TT instead of cold 4-betting. So his cold 4-bet is the top only.
- **Unusually big opener** (30-35 when he normally opens 20-25): JJ or AK ("I don't want to see a flop") or AA/KK (greed). He's "not folding for any amount."
- **Habitual EP limper:** limps suited broadways and small pairs. When he raises, it's the top 5-7%.
- **Limp-reraiser:** AA, KK, AKs, maybe QQ. No bluffs.
- **BB rec closing the action vs an EP open:** just calls 99, TT and KQs (2024), because the passive line is his default. So his 3-bet is "better and best."
- **3-bettor in position vs our 4-bet from out of position:** calls a lot (ATs, KTs, AJs), so 4-bet linear hands that dominate those calls (2024; matches 2026's linear OOP rule).
- **Cold caller in a squeeze spot:** capped, and "much higher chance" someone calls, so expect a flop.
- **Tight rec open-limper** (2024): limps pairs, suited broadways and suited Ax, and raises only premiums.
- **Two passive fish in the blinds** (2024): they rarely 3-bet, call too much, and play draws passively. On the BTN, open nearly everything.
