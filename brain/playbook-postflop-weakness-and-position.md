## 4. Pounce on weakness
- **Repeat small bets mean a weak range** (77, 88, 9x). Raise big and take it [08-25].
- **Over-stabbers:** check-raise your high-equity draws. On the turn, raise their repeat small bet big [08-25].
- **When his line caps him**, size up or overbet. With a range fold available, bet huge: 400 into 110 with 9-high vs an old tight rec [09-15]. Capped on the river with no brick wall: "Empty the clip" [08-04].

- **Two players both capped on the turn** (one checked, one bet small into two): check-raise [08-04].
- **A rec double-barrels BIG: he has it. With trips, check-raise the turn [04-14].** He won't fold a queen. Check-calling and donking the river wins the minimum. "When they tell you twice, believe them."
- **"Tell him twice" vs a passive FOLDER [02-17]:** a lone river blast after you only called gets called by JJ/AT. Check-raise the turn, THEN fire the river. Even folders need two big signals for "the big boy folds." The river doesn't need to be egregiously big. **[2025-01-07 HHP] the same idea after YOU check back the flop** (BTN vs a rec BB, A-7-2 rainbow, T9s): his turn CHECK means no sets, two pair or AQ (they'd bet), so double-barrel BIG (his draws and 7x are inelastic and call once) and bet about 3x pot on the river. He bets SMALL on the turn: float the whole range, even a gutshot; if he bets small again on the river raise, and if he checks the river bet 3.5x-5x pot (no need to be efficient, you won't meet strong hands, and it looks like the AA/77/22 traps). He bets BIG on the turn: fold, and don't bluff the river even if he checks (recs telegraph with size). Whether to c-bet A72 at all is the ♣2 question in postflop section 2. **[2025-10-28]** the same idea vs a rules nit ("submits to authority"): turn check-raise, then a river bet, "in no uncertain terms." He also never triple-barrels, so float his flop and turn wider. **[2025-12-23]** a rec's bet-bet-check line is value-heavy: one big bet won't fold his AA/AK, so jam your value and only bluff Q-high SMALL, to fold combo draws.

If he calls, shut down unless you improve. *(Marc's numbers from his own solver screen.)*

- **A c-bettor who splits sizes [2024-12-31 HHP, 2025-01-21 HHP]** (supporting; agrees with the wet-board small c-bet line in section 6). His small size holds too many weak hands and his big size too many strong ones. BB vs BTN on J-9-2, no showdown value (78hh): check-raise vs the small size to about 2.5x-3.5x (no need to go "balls to the wall"), fold most hands vs the big size, and with a strong hand (J9s) check-raise BIG, don't slow-play. Same in a 3-bet pot [2025-01-21 HHP] (a BTN who stabs TT small and bottom set big): range check OOP, check-raise every hand with no showdown value but a shred of equity (9h6h, 7d6d, gutshots) vs the small stab, overfold showdown value (99, TT, JJ, ace-high) vs the big one, and pile in with 88/QQ/KK/AA. If he checks the flop back his range is crippled: bet the turn big with air and jam the river. *(Solver numbers, like TT checking back about 80%, are the coach's own screen.)*
  - **A c-bettor who uses ONE size [2024-12-31 HHP]:** he under-double-barrels, so float the flop with anything with equity, fold the turn to a bet, and if the turn checks through with no showdown value overbet-bluff the river. With ace-high, check and bluff-catch.
  - **A rec who fast-plays value and check-calls his draws [2025-01-21 HHP]** (raises a set, just calls with AhQ): overfold bluff-catchers (99, TT) to his check-raise, stack him with top set, and if you check-call the flop your bluffs print on later streets. C-bet your whole range even on bad boards, but size down or check on flush and straight turns, since his passive draws got there.
- **Live players over-stab, telegraph strength with size, and don't protect their check-back range** [08-11, 08-18, 09-15].
- **"Hold the logical thread" [08-11, 09-15]:** once you decide he's passive, capped or donks his flushes, size EVERY street off that read. **Same idea, older [2024-10-08 HHP, 2024-12-31 HHP]:** if he calls a river jam with 77, jam your value (88, T9s, TT) thin and stop bluffing; if he folds it, bluff. "Stay logic-consistent": don't bet 500 as a bluff and jam for value (QQ vs a player who folds Tx to a jam gets a chunky 400-600; the jam is the bluff). Example: QJs jam-bluffs a K river after his showdown hands (77, A4) called the turn.
- **Read donks by SIZE [06-23]:**
  - A big donk (about pot) is top pair or an overpair. Raise LARGE for value.
  - A half-pot donk is "weak marginal" stuff. Just call, even with a set, and let him keep betting.
  - A small turn donk folds to a raise. Raise small, then bet small on the river when he checks.
  - **Context decides whether a donk is weak or strong [2024-10-08 HHP].** Three-bet pot, A-Q-2 board, we bet small and he called: his $230 turn lead at 5/5 is strong (players put you on AK, you've shown two aggressive actions, and the bet is big for the game), so fold. The same lead is weak in a small, wide-vs-wide pot with a small bet and no aggression before it (BTN opens 84, BB calls, A-Q-2, he leads $40). Judge the pot type, the earlier aggression, the board and the size together.
  - **The same read, earlier video [2025-05-20 HHP, the same "Debbie Donk" hands]:** her small donk is a weak range (K-x, 4x, 3x, gutshots, pocket fives) and her strong hands check-raise. Flop: CALL (a raise makes her fold), with air AND with a set. Turn small donk: raise to about 120 (a call-then-raise "tells the story" better than a flop raise). River: about half pot (quiz answer 175; 250-300 also works), so K6/K7/K10 call. Her sizing is face up: bigger when improved. A big donk (about pot, $50): raise to about 6x ($300, not $150), because she's sticky and waited for a safe flop with 99/TT.
- **He showed he plays flush draws fast** (check-raises them, stacks off with naked draws), then just calls and the flush comes in: he's capped. Go BIG on that turn, not the usual small nut-changing size [06-16, 06-23]. Bet-3-bet thin vs his check-raises (top pair, overpair, NFD) [06-16].
- **The flush comes in after you barrel [06-16, 2025-04-22 HHP, 2025-04-08 HHP, 2025-05-06 HHP]:**
  - He donks big: fold bluff catchers, even a set.
  - He CHECKS: he has no flush and he's elastic. Jam your bluffs (big overbet bluffs with bricked draws, bet thin value with Qx/two pair). Vs a player who doesn't donk his flushes, a check tells you nothing: no jam, at most a small bluff [2025-05-06 HHP].
- **Flop checked back by a player who checks back the nuts [06-16]:** he's uncapped. Use a smaller turn check-raise and a moderate river, and don't play for stacks. When that player c-bets instead, he's capped: check-raise big on wet boards, smaller on dry ones.
  - *Superseded by 06-16:* [2025-04-22 HHP] (a) check-raise the SMALL size vs his c-bet because we check-raise 30-50% of the range; (b) after a flop check-through with the nuts or a strong hand, overbet 2x-3x or check and check-raise big, never half to 3/4 pot (he isn't folding sets, two pair, TT or A9 even to a big bet, so 15/20/65 makes no difference).
- **A flop-aggressive check-raiser (raises both value and open-enders) [2025-04-22 HHP]:** he's capped on blank AND nut-changing turns, since he raised his draws. C-bet only the easy call vs a check-raise (K-x, two pair, nut flush draws, combo draws) and the easy folds (air); check back the in-between hands (AJ, A8, 8x). Size up on flush and straight turns with value and bluffs and plan to empty the clip on the river. With a nutted hand (bottom set) vs his check-raise, consider just calling; vs a typical live player who over-raises strong hands, 3-bet.
- **He c-bets BIG multiway [2025-04-22 HHP]:** his range "may be too strong" (he put two pair in the big size). Overfold anything worse than top pair good kicker, call with draws for implied odds, and fast-play a set with a massive check-raise (live players don't fold overpairs or sets to one raise). His SMALL multiway c-bet is too weak: check-raise anything with a shred of equity. *(Superseded by 06-16 on the overfold line: 06-16 folds even top pair to a big multiway c-bet plus an overcall.)*

**Multiway pots, Marc's system [2025-08-12, *95% of Pots Are Multiway (The System That Fixes Your Winrate)*, mostly 2/5; his on-screen flowchart was checked against the spoken hands].** Marc says multiway pots are 95% of what you face. These rules are for multiway SRPs.
- **As the preflop raiser: "Is it likely to get stabbed?"** Likely = a wet board (flush/straight draws), a dynamic board (the nuts can change; 7-4-2 rainbow is dry but dynamic) and more players left to act behind you. On a dry, static board players may check back Ax.
  - **Likely to get stabbed:** BET thick value only (TPTK, an overpair: even here, a deviation from his 5/5 challenge, because check-raising them "value owns" yourself and bet-bet-bet gets called by worse). CHECK CPFS (can play for stacks, hands that cooler strong hands) and high-equity draws, so you can "big boy" check-raise the stab and play a two-street game. Also check thin value, showdown value, low-equity draws and air (the chart).
  - **Not likely to get stabbed** (dry, static, fewer players behind): BET CPFS, thick value and high-equity draws, because "we need to start putting in the money ourselves." CHECK thin value, low-equity draws and air. (The chart doesn't list showdown value in this check box [unclear].)
  - **Examples:** 66 on a wet flop: check, and check-raise a stab. 88 middle set, 4-way: check, "big boy" check-raise, jam most clean turns. AJ on J-8-7: bet-bet-bet even though it's likely to be stabbed. 99 on A-9-2 with one player behind: bet.
- **Multiway raises the bar.** KJ on J92 is three streets heads-up (half pot flop, overbet turn, 400-500 river to target Jx) but a two-street hand four-way, and only two streets if the SB calls next to act and everyone else folds. Check the crappy gutshot and weak flush draws (65 of spades) multiway. Bet good straight draws that cooler. ⚖ Good flush draws and combo draws, two dated views (see the draw bullet below). Heads-up you can empty the clip with a naked gutshot, unless he check-raises.
  - **⚖ Multiway good flush draws and combo draws, two dated views (Joan 2026-09-29: not confirmed to be the same spot, so NOT collapsed; no winner):**
    - **[2025-08-12 HHP, *95% of Pots Are Multiway*]:** KJ on J92 board, 4-way (flush-draw board). Bet good flush draws that cooler, plus good straight draws and combo draws (pair + flush draw, straight + flush draw). Check weak flush draws and crappy gutshots.
    - **[2026-04-21 HHP, *20 Question Multiway Quiz*]:** 4-way wet flop, checked to you (the hand was 87dd; board not spoken in captions). A good but non-nut flush draw (K-high or A-high) or a combo draw is the WORST draw to bet. Don't bloat the pot with non-nut draws: big reverse implied odds, someone likely hits better.
    - What differs: the boards (J92 vs an unspoken wet flop), the action (2025-08-12 not stated vs checked to you), and the wording ("good" flush draw vs "good but non-nut"). The combo-draw advice is the direct opposite. Flag: the 2026-04-21 spot may be the newer answer for the same hand class; Joan hasn't ruled.
  - **The value bar, same video:** 4-way on a wet flop, JT is the worst hand to bet (QJ/KJ maybe); if the SB calls, maybe only AJ+ has three streets. If only the limper calls and the blinds fold, go thinner (QJ becomes an easy three-street bet). On a static A-high flop 4-way, AK is about the worst value hand (AJ/AT won't get three streets, since recs put you on AK). Heads-up, AJ/AQ can be the worst ace. The threshold rises the more players are in.
- **Size by what each hand wants:** CPFS on a wet board = small (1/4 to 1/3 pot, where heads-up he'd bet half pot), because the absolute pot is bigger and you want strong hands to raise and weak ones to call. Thick value (KK) = a big bet, for max value vs AQ and A-high flush draws, since KK gains nothing from being raised. Using small with everything "is okay, too." **[2026-04-21 HHP] the same split with dollars:** nut straight on a wet flop with ONE player behind ($35 into a ~$100 pot, CO opens and three call): small, since players fast-play two pair, sets and monster draws on wet boards and thin draws keep calling. Dry static flop (99 on A-9-2, one player behind): BIG ($90 into ~$100), because players don't fast-play there and you must put the money in yourself. KK overpair on Q-7-6: c-bet about $60 (size up with non-nutted hands; a bit smaller with 77/66, which want a raise) and fold to a big turn donk.
- **PFR bluff multiway:** only on boards unlikely to be stabbed, over multiple streets and BIG, holding cards that block the NUTTED hands (K5 on a dry static flop blocks 55/A5/AK; the sticky AJ/AT/A8 call flop and turn, then fold the river). Do NOT hold J, T or Q, which block the AJ/AT you want to fold. This is the rare spot where blockers matter, "unlike 99%" of others. A5dd with bottom pair on a board that might be stabbed: don't c-bet as a bluff; if it checks through you often see a free turn, a big stab = fold, a small stab = consider a bluff. **[2026-04-21 HHP] the same K5s example:** $90 big, big turn, jam, and only if they fold AJ/AT/A8 by the river (he says most do at 200bb+). If you believe they'd never fold AJ/AT, bet-bet-jam every AJ+ for value instead: "if you don't, you don't really believe it." Check A5s (bottom pair) when two players are behind, and fold it to a cutoff bet plus a button call: two uncapped ranges make a horrible check-raise bluff.
- **As the caller, run from:** an aware player's multiway c-bet (a good pro c-betting into three players: KQ+ and big draws; fold JJ, consider folding QJs/KQs). A big multiway c-bet from a strong-opening limper (Limping Larry opens 5x UTG and bets pot: KK/QQ/JJ; fold an 88 overpair). A multiway c-bet plus a call from the next player to act ("super strong"): fold top pair with nothing else. **Pounce** with a turn check-raise when the other players are capped (the flop checked through, the CO and BTN checked back): only the BB is uncapped, so with A2s don't "monkey stab" the turn, check, and if the CO stabs check-raise and turn nearly everything that doesn't beat second pair into a bluff.
  - **More folds [2026-04-21 HHP]:** JJ underpair to a pro's c-bet into three players (closer if you're last to act with the others folded: one float); 88 vs Limping Larry's pot-size bet (a rec's multiway pot bet = TT+); 89cc top pair no kicker vs an LJ half-pot c-bet plus a BTN call (needs a "massive overlay"); a next-to-act caller is a "big red flashing warning sign."
  - **Who is capped [2026-04-21 HHP]:** after a check-through, the player who checked (you) is the ONLY uncapped one; the others bet their strong hands once the PFR checked (an LJ raiser is capped more than 75% of the time on wet boards). After a bet and a call, the bettor and the next-to-act caller are uncapped and the BB is capped (he raises his strong hands like 55/KT). Compare red flag 2 below (2025-08-12): a multiway check-back is not weak, it holds pot-control hands like TT/99/KQ.

- **Why check:** the old stable default was a small c-bet, which led to turns where they had showdown value, faced a stab and had "no information." The IP player over-stabs, telegraphs with his size, and a check-back is "weak, crippled, and capped." Checking lets him make his mistakes.
- **Flop, question 1: "Is it likely to get stabbed?"** Yes (wet/dynamic board, or an aggressive villain) = check the ENTIRE range. Dynamic vs static matters more than wet vs dry. No (dry/static) = play more passively (question 2). Even on a static board, vs a super-aggro "monkey stabber," checking the range "may be good."
- **Flop, question 2: "Is SPR over 15?"**

- **Flop, Q1: "Is it likely to get stabbed?"** (wet or dynamic board, or an aggressive villain: yes). YES: check the ENTIRE range, even at 100bb, then check-raise the stab, and play a one- or two-street game. Example: 99 (set) on J-9-2 with two spades: check, check-raise the stab, "pile in all the money" across the flop and turn; a lead alone "wouldn't be able to get in all the money." He is very likely to stab unless he's the most passive of players, and his size telegraphs his strength; if he checks back, he "just doesn't have very many strong hands." If the flop checks through, apply pressure on later streets vs a range that "can't withstand heat."
- **Flop, Q2 (NO, the board is dry/static): "Is SPR over 15?"** Rule of thumb: an SRP 200bb+ deep is over 15, 150bb or less is under. Measure with the EFFECTIVE stack (his).
  - **SPR over 15 ($975 effective, $57 pot):** BET only thick value (AK on A-7-2: bet-bet-bet). CHECK CPFS (77 checks, to cooler A7/A2/22/AK/AQ and play for stacks), thin value, showdown value, draws and air. "It is just not viable" to get it all in with AK, which would be "value owning ourselves." Air (JTcc) checks and applies pressure on the turn and river after he checks back.

Bet-bet-bet, "never putting in an overbet," and a river bet of less than 75% pot still gets it in; his AJ/AT/A9 don't fold. Betting "is the way to minimize fold equity," so no check-raise is needed.

- **Facing a flop stab, BIG (over about 2/3 pot):** a strong range ("10, 15, 20% too much strong stuff"). XR = CPFS (88 on Q-8-4) and combo draws (JThh, T9hh). - **Facing a flop stab, SMALL, STATIC/DRY board (K-8-4 rainbow, 200bb):** XR ONLY CPFS and combo draws, "very similar" to facing a big stab; don't go "balls to the wall" with low-equity bluffs. ## 6. Bluffing: air vs showdown value
Todd's #3 leak (an HHP student, not Joan): turning showdown value into bluffs in position [09-15].
- **No showdown value on the river: almost always bluff. The question is size, not whether [07-07].**
  - Vs a strong range, "the scalpel": a small "baby bet" folds his draws and A/K/Q-high, even though the nuts snap you off.
  - Vs a capped range (top pair at best), "the sledgehammer": empty the clip.
  - Checking back wins $0. Live players are "so terrified to get stacked." Getting stacked sometimes is fine.
- **A bluff WITH showdown value has to beat what checking back wins** [09-15]. With 44 in a $60 pot, checking back is worth about $15-18.
- **The test: "Can I get a range fold?"** If not, check back [09-15].
- **"Imaginary showdown value" [04-14].** If you'd fold the river, you had no showdown value on the turn, so barrel the turn. Both can't be true. A-high in a 3-bet pot, or third pair vs a UTG check-caller on an A-high board, usually has none. Look at his range, not your pair.
  - **[2024-12-10 HHP] "the showdown fallacy" and "don't give up on rivers" (supporting).** BTN 76dd vs a rec BB, J-8-4 flop, turn double-barrelled, river 7 gives third pair and he checks: third pair has no real showdown value (he has QJ/JT/J9 top pair, straights and spade draws). Test: what size gets his Jx to call? Under pot, so a NEAR-POT bet (175-200 into about pot) folds every Jx, and the bluff beats checking back a hand that beats nothing. Same in a second spot with 7-high on a 10-5-4-K-10 board where a small flop bet let many draws in: bet the river very small. A bad river card for you is not a reason to check.
- **Turn showdown value into a bluff when nothing worse takes his line [01-27].** 3-bet pot, the flush turn checks through, the board pairs, and he bets small IN position: that's thin value (AK, KQ, KJ). Check-raise ATs big, the size value would NOT use. Third pair where the draws got there "beats nothing": bet. Checking back wins "$2 of this pot." *(Same tests as 09-15 and 05-12: beat what checking back wins, and only vs a thin-value or capped range.)*
  - **[2024-12-17 HHP] agrees on the bluff, and adds a call-the-raise line.** BTN 3-bets KQs vs a rec's open, the rec calls, flop Q-J-10 with two hearts, the rec check-raises after a 1/3-pot bet, we call, and the rec checks the turn: no flush (he'd keep betting it), so his line reads as straights, sets and the occasional two pair, and our top pair (with an open-ender) has no showdown value. Bet the turn as a bluff (about 2,000-2,600 into about 3,200; at these stakes "dollars speak more than big blinds," so no big river SPR is needed) and shove a blank river.
  - **⚖ CONFLICT: Joan to decide. - **Don't bluff UNCAPPED ranges [01-27].** In-position callers slow-play much more than out-of-position players. After a c-bet and a double barrel they have "a ton of two pair," so by the river you're "in jail": big gets snapped by two pair, small by an ace. A flop check-raiser who checks a pairing turn is trapping full houses. Fix it earlier: pick a line that caps him (e.g. a tiny turn bet on flush or pairing turns so his flushes raise, then the scalpel).

- **A paired-top-card river is a bad bluff spot vs an aware, sticky player [04-14]:** Qx and trips don't fold. (Matches 08-11.)
  - **[2025-12-23] the same board, the fix is SIZE:** in a 3-bet pot, the river pairs the top card (K) and his range is lots of Kx trips plus busted spade draws. Bluff T-high/J-high SMALL, "very small," only to fold Ax/Qx/Jx of spades. A jam just loses to Kx. "Scalpel, not jackhammer." This also beats the jam at low SPR (about 125bb).
- **Before a big bluff, find the fold threshold [2025-12-16].** Marc's example, from his own nodelock: a river jam vs a capped top-pair range (T4o on a K-high board, holding the T that blocks QT) makes about +$80 if he folds top pair 75%, and loses about $500 if he folds it only 10%. The jam only works if he folds top pair about 60%+. "Be pretty damn sure your opponent is capable of folding top pair." Vs a sticky player, don't.

**How big to bluff: fold equity and depth [2024-10-01 HHP, *Here's Why Your Bluffs Aren't Working*; 2025-02-18 HHP; 2024-12-31 HHP; 2024-12-10 HHP].** These are the oldest bluff-sizing lessons. They agree with the later "uncapped or capped" ideas in this section; nothing below contradicts an earlier ruling.
- **The rule [2024-10-01 HHP]:** the more fold equity you have, the less equity your hand needs. Fold equity is high when he is CAPPED (his earlier actions took the strong hands out, since he'd have raised them) AND you are DEEP; it is low when he is uncapped or you're shallow. Flop question: will he raise strong and call weak? If yes, bet small a lot; if no, bet big or check.
- **Capped and deep (BTN vs BB, Q-9-7 two-tone, small flop bet, blank turn):** bet BIG with air and low-equity draws (54dd) on the turn, then jam the river at about 2.5x pot ("empty the clip"). With ATs a big turn is fine too, since he won't fold a queen anyway.
- **Uncapped turn (a flush card):** bet SMALL with everything, air included, so he raises his flushes and keeps his weak hands; once he is capped (his best hand is AQ) blast the river BIG. Old-video version of the same idea [2024-12-10 HHP]: a rec who checks after a flop check-raise when the board pairs = mostly two pair, sets and boats, so bet small on the turn (60-80 into about 260), then tiny on the river (a quarter to a fifth of the pot); with the flush-completing turn instead bet BIG (150 into about 185) and keep a jam behind, because he never has the flush and this is the one spot two pair and sets fold (only when you're deep). On a nut-changing paired turn bet small with value AND bluffs (30-35 into 85, not 70). The sizing rules are in postflop section 2.
- **An in-between turn (a jack on Q-9-7 two-tone):** high-equity hands (ATs) go BIG (they need little fold equity); pure air (54dd) goes small to re-cap him. If "capped" or "deep" isn't fully true, blasting with air "starts to become a punt."
- **Shallow, or dry and uncapped (A-9-7 rainbow):** shallow, top pair won't fold, so use tiny river bets only to fold draws and 9x/7x and check back showdown value (ATs). On a dry, static board he may trap, so bet BIG or check the flop, and bluff only with the highest-equity hands (T8dd) or with 7x blockers when deep (76dd, 75ss: 225bb+, overbet-bet-jam). Uncapped and shallow is "maybe the worst spot to bluff."
- **Depth is the big adjustment [2025-02-18 HHP]:** at 100bb do NOT try to make him fold top pair (nobody folds AJ/KJ/QJ for about $375 behind). At 200bb top pair folds on the river. IP on a static ace-high board: no range bet; at 200bb bottom pair (44, A4) can run a triple-barrel overbet bluff to fold AQ/AJ/AT (a 4 blocks strong combos here); at 100bb check back, take showdown or bluff with jack-high if he checks the turn, and don't turn bottom pair into a bluff if he bets. Wet board IP, small c-bet called, he's capped: 200bb very large turn and empty the clip on blank rivers; 100bb bet small to keep his weak range in then bluff a small river ($40-60), or bet big and size down on brick rivers. Rivers vs a capped villain: 200bb bluffs bigger, value smaller (99 for $400-500, not 2x); 100bb bluffs small, value bigger. (Deep-stack sizes are in `playbook-deep-stacks.md`.)
- **Double-barrel turns with air vs recs even with no equity [2024-12-10 HHP]:** recs call too wide and raise their strong hands on the flop, so the turn range is weak and crippled. Equity is the "get out of jail free card"; the less you have, the more fold equity you need, and recs give it. If you won't, check back more air on the flop.
- **The 2025 pool folds top pair on rivers [2024-12-31 HHP]:** so triple-barrel and jam-bluff with your non-showdown hands (a jam bluff folds "80, 85, 90%"), and as the caller massively overfold rivers (the pool under-triple-barrels by "3, 4, 5, maybe 10x"). This asks the same thing as the [2025-12-16] fold-threshold test above: be sure he folds top pair. Depth matters: it is a 200bb-plus read, not a 100bb one. [2025-06-17 HHP] (below) splits the same question by player: the loose or aggressive player is the one to bluff, and the tight or passive one under-folds top pair. 2024-12-31 states the pool read without that split; both dated views stand.
- **Vs a player who overfolds rivers [2025-01-21 HHP]:** one who folds A6 to a river shove (the solver calls it 50-65%) is overfolding, so jam every hand with no showdown value (AQhh, AJhh, A4hh), even with bad blockers, and chain the exploit from the flop. "If a player is just slightly overfolded, take every hand with no showdown value and pile in money." He also splits river sizes: small = his bluffs (call with showdown value), just under pot = his value (overfold, even 77/99). Check back showdown-value hands on the turn so you can bluff-catch his small river bet.

**When to empty the clip: his range must be weak at the river [2025-05-06 HHP, *They Thought I Was Tight. I Made $1M Bluffing Them Blind.*].** The rule: if he arrives with a STRONG range and must fold strong hands for the bluff to work, be careful; if he arrives WEAK and is only "supposed to" call weak pairs, empty the clip. Live players make the big folds far less often than the weak-range calls, so the bluff you should fire is the one vs a range that has no strong hands to fold. (The coach's example A is muddled in the captions [unclear]; use the rule, not the example.)
- **Weak-range signs:** the check-caller who would have raised his sets, two pair and straights earlier (capped); a villain who calls flop AND turn with too many weak hands (pair plus gutshot, second pair) and "talks himself into it": he arrives at the river too wide and weak. - **Wet, dynamic board, a typical player who raises J9/99/22 on the flop:** overbet the turn and empty the clip on the river, since the best hand he reaches the river with is Jx.
- **Sizing the turn decides it:** on a top-card-paired flop with an overcard to bottom pair on the turn, go small again so he arrives wide and weak. A big turn folds his 5x and small pairs and leaves tens, jacks, trips and boats (section 2).

Delineating weak from strong river ranges makes "80-90-95%" of your bluffs get through. Vs a non-donker who checks a flush river: no jam, at most a small bluff.

**Small river bets and thin bluffs [2025-06-10 HHP]**
- **IP vs a capped villain, bet SMALL on the river,** for thin value (one pair) and as a small bluff to fold missed draws. A big size folds out the hands you want, and live players don't check-raise thinly or as a bluff. (A9hh on a 9c river after a turn 7 small bet: small. 87dd with a big turn double barrel and a K river: about 200 to fold A-high/Q-high/J-high/10-high draws, not trying to fold trips.)
- **A big-boy river jam even with bad blockers** (KJ of clubs) vs a weak, capped range that raised its strong hands earlier.
- **Ridiculously thin freeroll value:** a capped, elastic rec who bluff-check-raised the small turn and can't beat your 6: bet $50 into 342 on the river to induce A-high calls or a spaz.
- **Overbet a flush turn after the flop checks through** (KJhh: 125 into 57, then 250 on the river so an ace still calls); check again on blank turns.
- **Wet-board small c-bets:** vs a BTN who c-bets about 80% and splits sizes, a small size = a too-weak range: check-raise everything with no showdown value (small size, lots of hands, even without backdoors). Be more careful on static boards (A72, A92, K73).
- **A pro (not a fish) who bets small on a flush river after a big turn:** check-raise all-in as a bluff (he splits sizes, so strong hands would bet bigger; his small bet is flushes that fold). Don't try it vs fish, who never fold a flush.
- **Donk the river with the nuts** vs passive players who check back everything (AK/88/77 don't fold to a pot-size lead, and they don't bluff enough for you to miss anything by checking).
