#### The 9 live player types [2025-10-28] (2/5 with a 1K cap and 5/10 examples; 200bb or 300bb when stated)
Marc's frame: don't decide your style before you see the table, and "play like water." His nicknames for the types are jokes; the plain names are used here.

**Jackpot old-man-coffee (OMC), a tight rec 3-bettor**
- *How to spot:* waits for the bad-beat jackpot, "if you buy in for more than the max you'll kill the jackpot," "back in my day."
- *Range:* massively under-3-bets ("may not even 3-bet AK all the time"). His big bet on a dynamic board is AA/KK/QQ/JJ, with few AK/AQ in the big size. He opens, calls 3-bets and calls streets "too tight." "Almost never" double- or triple-barrel bluffs.
- *vs a check:* bets big with overpairs. *vs a check-raise:* won't fold an overpair to one raise. *vs a small river bet after your big turn:* his missed flush draws (AQhh, AJhh) fold "if we breathe on the pot"; his pairs call a big bet.
- *Size:* fold TT to his near-pot flop bet on 7-6-2 (exception: if he can 3-bet AQs/AK, fire once and give up, "maybe floating one time is okay"). Check-raise your value (you lose no bluffs, he doesn't barrel). Turn big, then a SMALL river bluff, only to fold his flush draws. "Don't need to empty the clip." Dial back bluffs overall.

**"GTO wizard" (memorizes, can't think for himself)**
- *How to spot:* "I'm at the top of my range, I can't fold," "I unblocked his continue range," "that size isn't a thing in equilibrium." At 2/5-5/10 he "talks a big game" but doesn't really understand theory.
- *Range:* after he 3-bets and you call: AA, AK, KQ, KJ at a high frequency, and "almost never" a flush at 300bb (he doesn't check back flushes on the turn). After a flop check-back on a wet board, the strong hands would have bet the flop, so his Ax (A7s, A4s) is the strong part. He stabs all his air on an ace turn. In a 4-bet pot he likely has QQ/JJ/TT.
- *vs a check:* value-bets thin on the river; stabs air and bets Ax for value on the turn. *vs a big bet:* your big turn check-raise gets called by AJ/AK, then he folds the river. A river check-raise folds AK/AA when he doesn't hold the flush. On low boards in 4-bet pots he expects 20-33% and makes calling mistakes vs a bigger size. In unfamiliar spots (a donk) he goes "into a more passive shell."
- *Size:* check the river, don't lead it ("accomplishes nothing"); if he bets, "big boy" check-raise as a bluff. Check the turn on the ace, then a big check-raise ("the absolute biggest size that they will still call" AJ/AK) and a river jam. Donk the nuts in a 3-bet pot. In 4-bet pots bet half pot to 60% on low boards. Drag him "to the jungle" and push side games. Marc warns: use equilibrium to see how he's supposed to respond, then check whether he does. Don't memorize it.

**Rules nit ("show one, show all")**
- *How to spot:* "show one, show all"; wants order, hates uncomfortable decisions, "submits to authority."
- *Range:* after a flop check-back on Q-5-4, A4/A5 are his strong hands, AK/AJ are Ax that checked, and 33/22/66 show up if he checks back the turn too. He can't triple-barrel: his draws give up the river, "check-check a lot."
- *vs a small turn lead:* Ax click-calls, then snaps off a river bluff. *vs a check-raise then a river bet:* folds "when told twice."
- *Size:* don't lead the turn; check, then a "big boy" turn check-raise and a river bet. After a turn check-back, bet the river at the size that folds pocket sixes. Float his flop and turn wider: call the turn with 99 if he gives up the river most of the time (Marc would fold it to the big flop stab 90%+; his solver screen: a pure fold at equilibrium, a pure call worth about $55-59 vs this type). He also "combusts" when the game gets big and splashy.

**Tilter (plays 100% off his results today)**
- *How to spot:* mutters at the dealer, throws his cards.
- *By mood:* content or in a good mood, he under-bluffs and over-folds. Steaming after a big loss, he bluffs more and won't fold.
- *Size:* content = over-fold to his bets and over-bluff him with "massive bluff sizes." Steaming = hero-call, stop bluffing, "massive value sizes." Your image doesn't matter: Marc says that if the tilter isn't tilting he still triple-barrel bluffs him the very next hand, and he stops only after the tilter loses a big pot (AK losing to A7). "Always run it once" when the game allows (⚖ vs [07-07] in `playbook-game-mindset.md` section 3).

**Monster-under-the-bed folder**
- *How to spot:* flashes 66/A9s and mucks vs a late-position 3-bet, flashes top pair and mucks vs a c-bet, "I folded a flush, I knew I was behind."
- *Range:* calls the turn with pairs and draws (capped but inelastic), then folds his pairs on the river. At 300bb, after he check-raises and then checks a brick turn, he has 99/22/J9s (top of range) and no flush (he'd play flushes passively). A small bet from him (250) on a 4-flush river isn't the nuts; the flush would bet "absolutely massive."
- *vs a big bet:* folds even the Ah. AT "folds the river all the time."
- *Size:* bluff everything, even with bad blockers (a QJhh triple barrel, jam the river). Float wet flops wide. At 300bb: float his flop check-raise, turn "really really big," river "empty the clip." With top set, turn it into a bluff and jam over his small 4-flush river bet ("care less about your line making sense"). Never show a bluff; say "good fold," and show your value. Marc maybe sizes down "a little" on the turn vs this scared type.

**Ego player ("I usually play bigger")**
- *How to spot:* loves preflop wars, the money means less to him.
- *Range:* 3-bets too light and too much; calls 4-bets in position with 87s, 88, A5s, expecting to outplay you. Bricked flush draws and T9/97/T7 triple-barrel as bluffs.
- *vs a big bet:* calls too much ("the money just means less to them").
- *Size:* 4-bet LINEAR at 200bb (AJs, AQs, KQs, 99, TT, JJ). Don't make big hero folds preflop. River all-in for value (QQ/KK/AJ/AA). Don't run the "big boy bluffs" often. Bluff-catch with the checklist: hero-call wide vs wide (CO vs BTN, BTN vs BB). In tight vs tight spots (early vs early 3-bet pots) still overfold, even with two overs and a backdoor nut flush draw. (The 4-bet list checked against the CSV: `playbook-preflop.md` section 8.)

**Donker ("here to have a good time")**
- *Range:* a small donk (half pot or less) is rarely strong (survivorship bias: at showdown you see weak top or middle pair): weak pairs, some draws, mostly junk. A turn check after that is weak and elastic; another small donk is weak and elastic; a BIG turn donk is much stronger (99, a big draw, or improved).
- *Size:* heads-up SRP, small flop donk: FLOAT THE WHOLE RANGE (air like J9, nuts like 88, draws like T9cc). If he checks the turn, bet moderate with value AND bluffs. If he donks small again, raise small with bluffs (he may call with a draw, then fold the river). If he donks BIG on the turn: raise strong hands (33, 45) to set up a river jam, fold bluffs, call big draws. Multiway (3-way) or a big (80-90%) donk, or a donk in a 3-bet pot: don't float the whole range, be "very, very tight." ⚖ vs [01-20] on the big turn donk: postflop section 7.

**Trapper ("Phil Hellmuth")**
- *How to spot:* sunglasses, stares you down, "I can't believe you called me with that." He traps his strongest hands.
- *Range:* checks back QQ, 88, 55 (even AQ/AA), so his c-bet is unbalanced toward air. After he checks back the flop, he's NOT capped (77, 99, JJ, J9) and bets big on the turn with his traps. On a J-9-2 flop he may trap 22/99/J9. On a static K-9-2 board his check-raise is NOT 99/22/K9s, so Kx is his best hand and it folds by the river.
- *vs a small turn bet:* raises his strong stuff out of fear (a bad river, check-check, counterfeits). "They're not going to trap for three" streets.
- *Size:* check-raise his c-bets "willy-nilly" (no need for a big size). After his flop check-back: check-fold a lot; with a nutted hand overbet 3x-4x or check-raise massively. Blank turn: bet small again, then bluff the river if he only calls. Static K92, he check-raises: float, then raise the turn if he bets or bomb the turn and river if he checks; don't 3-bet the flop. ⚖ vs [06-16] (postflop section 4) and ↔ (postflop section 9).

**Whale (VPIP 70-80%+, isos about 80% of the time)**
- *How to spot:* "Dealer, can I bet this [Bentley key]?", and half the casino is on the table-change list for his table.
- *Range:* floats "incredibly way too wide" and is aggressive: over-stabs the flop and turn, then usually gives up the river (or takes "two aggressive actions"). Top pair can be a stack-off hand for him. He floats weak Tx, 99 and A-high for two streets, then folds them to a small river bet.
- *vs a big bet:* won't fold strong hands.
- *Size:* c-bet thick value OOP even large, check the turn, call the turn and river. With small showdown value: check-call flop and turn, fold a river bet. Small, small, small bluffs ("scalpel") and "absolutely huge, egregious" value ("bazooka"). The best seat is on his RIGHT (`playbook-game-mindset.md`). It fits [08-25] (bet big and overbet vs whales).

#### Reads from [2025-12-23] *Low Stakes Has Changed for 2026* (the general pool)
**BB defender who checks a paired river after you checked back the turn** (BTN vs BB, second pair)
- *Range:* few boats (they'd check-raise the wet flop) and few Qx (he'd bet trips after your check-back), lots of 6x, 5x, 77, 88, 99, few missed draws (he'd bluff after your check-back).
- *vs a big bet:* capped but inelastic, "just not folding even to a big size" with A6/88. *Size:* overbet 1.5-2x pot.

**Q-high 3-bet pot, CO opener who called your 3-bet**
- *Range:* AQ sometimes, QJ, QT, Q9s, JJ/TT sometimes, T9s, Ax of hearts. *vs a big bet:* inelastic on the turn, and on the river "doesn't fold." Bottom-card-pairing rivers make them "more sticky."
- *Size:* turn 250-300, river all-in for value, small bluff only to fold Ax of hearts/T9.

**BTN opener who checks back a static dry A-high flop** (even recreational players do)
- *Range:* lots of weak Ax (A3, A4, A5s, A7), plus K-high and 44 that fold to any bet. The Ax doesn't fold "even for a large size."
- *Size:* overbet the turn and river with top pair (⚖ vs check-raise: postflop section 5).

**Rec who bets big flop, big turn, then checks the river (UTG opener, we flatted on the BTN)**
- *Range:* AK, KQ, AA, KJ "at a very, very high frequency," sometimes JJ, big combo draws (ATcc, AQcc, Qxcc, A4cc); QT gives up.
- *vs a big bet:* checks to check-call with AA/AK/KJ "even if we put all the chips in." Combo draws can fold to a small bet.
- *Size:* jam value. Bluff Q-high small. To fold strong hands you must "tell them twice."

**Pool caller on a 4-straight / 4-flush river**
- *Range:* calls with much more than the straight: ATcc, two pair, A6cc, even 5x. Sticky: thinks "straight or nothing." *Size:* value "egregiously large," don't jam bluffs. Players who DO fold here: size down with value, up with bluffs.

**Trappy BB (the Hellmuth-type)** *(the full trapper entry is in the nine-types block above)*
- *Range and sizes:* 30-40% pot on a blank turn (he raises or calls and leads the river). On a flush turn he raises some flushes but his sets and two pair just call, and sets stop raising once the flush comes. Big overbet: he traps again and snaps off the river jam.
- *Size:* blank turn: small again; river big bluff only after check-call, check. Flush turn with air: check back; he bets the river: fold; he checks: bluff.

**Any type, on a static dry A72 as the PFR**
- *Range:* traps 77, 22, A7, A2; passive BB flats AJ/AQ/AK; lots of Ax (AT, A8, A9, A5, A4, A6).
- *vs a check (after your flop check-back):* bets big on the turn with AQ/AJ/77; a check is weaker. *vs a big bet:* the bigger you bet, the more he slowplays; top pair needs about 2x pot to fold and AJ/AQ/AK don't.
- *Size:* check back the flop; if he checks the turn, bluff big on turn and river. (♣ #2, postflop section 2.)

**3-bet pot, top card pairs on the river** (CO 3-bet vs HJ, we barreled a gutshot)
- *Range:* lots of Kx trips, busted spade draws (Axss, Qxss, Jxss). *vs a small bet:* busted draws fold ("not that much money"). *vs a jam:* trips never fold. *Size:* bluff T-high/J-high very small.

**BB defender vs a flush-completing river, Jx heavy** (SRP, we overbet the turn)
- *Range:* flushes and straights (uncapped), lots of Jx (AJo, KJo, QJo, J9s, J8s). *vs a small bet:* Jx calls about half pot. *vs a big bet:* Jx folds; flushes and straights call a jam.
- *Size:* value about half pot (200-250), bluff 300-350. Don't jam the bluff.

**Aggressive BTN 3-bettor who calls too much IP when deep** (KJ, KT, QT, QJ, Q9s; NOT super-passive OMCs)
- *Range on an A-high 4-bet-pot flop:* after a 1/4-1/3 c-bet only AK/AQ/AJ are left. A 10% bet keeps KK, QQ, JJ, TT and KJhh floating. On the turn he bets AK "mostly"; a check-back means few Ax.
- *vs a big bet:* river 300: TT-KK fold. *Size:* 4-bet linear (KQs, AQs, TT, JJ, AJs), not A5s; flop 10% pot, turn check, river bluff about 300. ⚖ vs Gethen [04-28 Gethen]: postflop section 2.

#### Reads from [2025-12-16] *This Mistake Cost Me $1 Million* (Marc's solver-study hands)
**Tryhard rec who fast-plays sets on a low, dynamic flop** (BB vs BTN, straddled)
- *Range on a river that pairs the deuce after a triple barrel:* Marc thinks most check-raise 55/66 on the flop, so on the river it's mainly 44. If he fast-plays 55/66 he also fast-plays 65s, which removes a main bluff. Solver bluffs are 65, 64, 75, some T6/J6.
- *vs a big river bet (overbet):* his river check-raise is "massively under-bluffed." *Size:* bet the river big to target sticky hands (76, A6, K6, 54), then FOLD to the check-raise.

**Rec BB with a capped range vs a straddle-pot c-bet and turn** (KJ2 flop, 7 turn, 9 river, we hold T4o)
- *Range:* he raised KJ/22 on the flop and has few if any 77 after calling. On the river: K9s and maybe QT, plus KQ, KT, some AJ, and missed heart draws.
- *vs a big turn bet:* inelastic, calls KQ, QT, Ax hearts. *vs a river jam:* solver has KQ folding about 50%, KT about 60%, K9s about 20%. Marc thinks KQ/KT "may fold." *Size:* turn 300; river jam only if he folds top pair 60%+ (see postflop section 6).

**Pool player who check-raises a small turn bet after you bet top pair** (KTss, EP opener vs the straddle)
- *Range:* the solver's bluffs are AJo/AQo with the Ad (AJ about 90%, AQ about 55% raise) and 76s, 86s, 64s, sometimes 53s/43s. The pool finds the Ad bluffs sometimes but "probably not" the 76/86/43s ones. Players are getting more aggressive and more aware of the nut-flush blocker.
- *Size:* bet small more often, and fold to the check-raise (a bluff catcher that beats no value is more profitable as a fold).

**Aggressive rec who check-raises the flop, then checks the turn** (blind vs blind, straddled)
- *Range:* 66/55/65 would keep betting the turn, so he's Jx (AJ, KJ, QJ) that slowed down plus showdown-value draws (78, 97). *vs a chunky turn bet:* AJ about 90% call, KJ the same, QJ folds 20-25%, 78/79 never fold (Marc thinks they fold even less). *Size:* about 250 on the turn.

**Aware player who checks back overpairs to call** (squeeze pot, T98 flop, a 8 turn gives us quads)
- *Range:* JJ+, AQs+, AKo, checks back AA/KK/QQ at a high frequency. *vs a lead-overbet:* the solver has AA/KK folding 55%, but Marc says "Is this happening? No, never." They over-call a lead and may fold to a check-raise. *Size:* lead-overbet 3,400 (⚖ vs check-raise: postflop section 5).

**Aware, incentivized bounty player** (winning 3 pots in a row = everyone pays $100-200)
- *Range:* over-c-bets the flop but won't fire three barrels indiscriminately. His turn overbet is probably UNDER-bluffed because he knows Marc calls down light. Thin value with big sizes: QJ, QT, 88, 78s, sometimes AA/KK. *vs a check-raise:* Marc thinks he folds QJ/QT, 88, 78s. *Size:* over-limp KT (he'll iso any two), call the flop, and a turn check-raise "starts to become interesting."

**Straddle who leads the turn, then bets 2x on a board-pairing river** (wide configuration)
- *Range:* flushes, trips (Kx); bluffs with missed straight draws (ATo with the As, 76, 64, 96, 97). At equilibrium some of those check (97 often, 96 pure, 64 sometimes, 76 10-15%). Flushes may size bigger (200-250) and Kx trips sometimes check.
- *Size:* call. Marc's nodelock (trips check 20% instead of 11%, flushes skip this size 15%): our fold drops from 43% to about 10%.

**Player who checks back Kx on a dry K-high flop, then bets big on a brick river** (KJ2, we hold QQ)
- *Range:* Kx (K8-KQ) that the solver checks back almost pure; solver bluffs are 65o/65s, 75, T5, 63s, 76o, T3s. His value density decides it. The pool bluffs bricked draws and air with SMALLER sizes, so a big size is under-bluffed. If he "monkey bets" every K on the flop he's light on value and easy to over-bluff. If he checks back Kx a lot he's easy to under-bluff. Final action not stated.

**Multiway player who stabs big on a straight-completing board** (4-way, OESD flop)
- *Range:* NOT 77/55/22/75s (they keep betting the turn). 88, 99, TT, A7s, 76s, and Qx of clubs (missed front-door flush). A big multiway stab indicates strength "much more than heads-up."
- *vs a river jam of about 3x pot:* solver TT folds ~88%, 99 ~85%, A7 and 76 100%, but Marc thinks the pool calls 88/99/TT/A7 MORE than equilibrium. *Size:* with the flush, jam for value; as a bluff, don't (88/99/Qx of clubs snap it off).

#### Reads from [2025-09-30] *90% of Money Is Won on the River* (2/5, $1K-1.5K effective)
**BB defender who fast-plays sets and two pair on a wet flop and vulnerable flushes on the turn**
- *Range:* sets and two pair raised the flop and 7-high or 8-high flushes raised the turn, so his river range is weak: 88, 99, Tx (AT, KT, QT).
- *vs a small bet:* Tx calls about 150 on the river. *vs a slightly bigger bet (about 200-250):* Tx folds. *Size:* value 150, bluff 220-285 (postflop section 2). Vs trappy players be careful with the bluff size; vs a fearful fast-player go a bit bigger.

**Live BB with Jx, river after bet-bet-bet by you** (SRP, A3ss vs BB, top pair on the ace river)
- *Range:* AJ, occasionally A9s that got sticky on the turn, A2ss/A4ss; Jx (KJ, QJ, JT); bricked K-high/Q-high flush draws.
- *vs a small bet:* Jx calls, bricked draws fold. A river check-raise after bet-bet-bet is "they're just going to have it." *vs a big bet:* two pair calls, Jx folds to about half pot. *Size:* value a quarter to a third of pot, bluff about half pot, sets jam.

**Sticky player from hours of history, 300bb** (stabs big with 77/44, never folds Qx)
- *Range:* AQ, 77, 44 on a Q-7-4 flop; A7/A4 "dubious" for a jam. Recs "don't tend to use large sizes for thin value. They just get scared."
- *Size:* check-raise the flop big, check the turn when the ace downgrades you, fold to his river jam with two pair.

**Player who c-bets his range, double-barrels and triple-barrels polar** (BTN opener, SRP, ace river)
- *Range:* AQ+, AT/A9, 6x trips, 78 straights, boats; missed 57, QJ, KJ, J8 (busted). JJ/QQ/Tx don't value-bet, and even A3/A4 may check back. "I have ace queen plus or basically I have nothing."
- *vs a raise:* he's not folding a straight. *Size:* call second pair "quite a bit," jam sets, min-click air (⚖: postflop section 7). Vs a THIN-value (linear) bettor the raise goes huge with bluffs.

#### Reads from [2025-10-21] *You're STILL Playing 3-Bet Pots Wrong* (Marc; 2/5, about $900 effective; the lessons are in postflop sections 2 and 5)
**IP player who called your 3-bet (general pool, you're the 3-bettor OOP)**
- *Range:* calls a small c-bet "too wide" and slow-plays some strong hands, then over-stabs the turn when you check. He telegraphs with his bet size and doesn't protect his check-back range: a check-back is "weak, crippled, and capped." IP players fast-play less than OOP players.
- *vs a small stab on a wet, dynamic board:* "far, far, far too weak." He massively under-double- and under-triple-barrels, and folds a lot to a check-raise.
- *vs a big stab ($150 into $205):* strong (sets and two pair, QJ/KJ that can fold sometimes), and he won't fold much to a check-raise.
- *Size:* see postflop section 5.

**Monkey stabber (super-aggro)**
- *Range:* stabs a lot even on a static board, but A9 and 77 are still in there (no need to bet big to protect on a static board).
- *Size:* check the range, or on a static board check-raise only CPFS and combo draws.

**Villain who calls your 3-bet and checks to you IP** (general pool)
- *vs a small c-bet:* raises strong hands, calls weak ones. *vs a big c-bet ($90-110):* traps strong hands "at a much higher frequency."
- *Static A-high board:* his Ax folds by the river because he puts you on AK, and the range is weighted to pairs 88-QQ. *Size:* c-bet small about 1/3 to 40% pot.

**Uncapped, slow-playing caller** (traps 99, 22, A9, A2, on a static dry flop)
- *vs a check on the turn:* bets big on the river with 99/22/A9. A river check caps him (9x/TT/JJ). *Size:* value big, showdown value checks, bluffs check and bluff the river only if he checks.

**Uncapped, fast-playing caller** (a flush card; he check-raises 8-high/Q-high flushes)
- *vs a small bet:* flushes raise, weak hands call. *Size:* small with value and bluffs. On a flush river: value small, bluff medium (450-500). Nitty Ned (never raises a flush, won't fold one to a big bet): value big.

**Capped, inelastic caller** (a wet flop where Q9/99/66 would have raised)
- *Range:* Qx, JT, A-high draws that never fold on the turn; on the river he bets a smaller size sometimes (bluff-catch). *Size:* value big (about 280), bluffs big but leave a river overbet (about 180). River: value jams, bluffs 250-280.

**Capped, elastic caller** (KK on a K turn, he checks)
- *Range:* 9x, A5s, TT, JJ, QQ, A9, A3, A4. Fold to a big bet (QQ and A9 fold, his river range becomes Kx+). Player-dependent: aggressive players fast-play 44/22 (capped), a super-passive player traps all his sets (uncapped). *Size:* small with value and bluffs on the turn. Deep river: value about pot (biggest size AQ/AJ/AT call), bluff jam.

#### Reads from [2025-09-16] *40+ Poker Tells* (how to read a player before and during the hand)
**By how he buys in** (his experience-based stereotypes, "your mileage may vary")
- *Reg/pro:* always tops up to the max, pulls big chips ($1k, $5k) out of a pocket, buys in with all $100 chips. More aggressive, thin value bets, empties the clip with bluffs. *Vs him:* dial exploits back (not balanced), give respect until he proves otherwise.
- *Fish:* buys in for less than the max, takes all $5 chips (10 stacks at 2/5), or buys in with $20 bills (a splashy action fish more than a passive nit, especially above the minimum). *Vs him:* play offense ("what do my two cards want to do vs this dummy"), no balance.

**Preflop and board reactions** (each is "not definitive," one more clue)
- *Quick preflop peel:* polar (AA/KK-type monsters or trash). If he quick-peels and then 3-bets, it can be the very top. *Long peel or a suit double-check:* the middle of his range (JTs, 88), not the top or bottom.
- *Looks away or down right after the turn or river is dealt:* the card likely helped him. *Stares at the board:* neutral at best.
- *Snap-call on the flop or turn:* the middle of his range (thin value, draws). Cut the top and the bottom. *Snap-bet when the nuts change:* polar. His medium hands would pause. Call a lot, raise huge with the nuts, click 2-2.2x with river air.
- *River, tanks 15-30 seconds, then checks:* weak. His range "can't withstand heat." Bluff big, size value down.

**By chips and posture**
- *Neat, lined-up stacks (nit):* too strong when he plays, won't fold strong hands. Don't pay him off, and bluff him less. *Big messy piles (action player):* gets to the river with wider, weaker ranges. Call down lighter, and if deep he may be a better player to bluff on the river.
- *Aggressive chip-toss when calling:* weak trying to look strong. *A discreet call with chips placed away:* could be very strong. "Wouldn't take these as gospel."
- *You reach to bet and he defensively grabs chips ("gold"):* "almost never" the top of his range, "99%" a weak hand. Value: size down "even smaller than you think." Bluff: go even bigger. *You count out chips to call and he lifts his cards as if to flip them:* most likely bluffing or missed draw. Bluff-catch lighter, raise value small.

**Misc table behavior** (stereotypes, "not 100% accurate")
- *Significant other sitting behind him, or he shows a losing hand for sympathy:* fish (slightly tighter/risk-averse with the partner). *Waits to post the BB after a break, or waits for the BB in an all-straddle game:* tighter, nittier. *Buys the button/BB instead of waiting:* more action, more thin value and bluffs.

**Table talk**
- "If you bet, I'm going to call": medium-to-weak showdown value that only calls "an egregiously small size." Bluffs big, value small.
- "I'm at the top of my range" (a "solver fish"): sees spots as nuts or bluff, hero-calls with blockers. Overbet thin value (top pair) at 2-3x pot.
- "Well, I don't have quads" with a flush on a paired board: eliminating value combos, so strong. "I guess it's time to go home": very strong. "AK is a drawing hand" [unclear]: a tight fish, his 4-bet is "almost always" AA. "I hate pocket jacks": fish. "I check blind," tips the dealer before the turn: fish. "Show one, show all": supernit, "never pay me off." "Anyone want a round of straddles?": action, good for the game. "AQ with the Qs or nothing" guy: rarely bluffs, go huge for thin value. "Screw you dealer": emotional, a nit when winning who spews after two bad beats.

**Trying rec BB on Q-7-4 with two spades, flush on the turn** [2025-01-28 HHP]
- *Range:* a flush "almost always" raises a small turn bet; small flushes (J-high, T-high, 9-high) slow down to a big bet ("check-call shell"). On the river with a straight-completing card he is capped at top pair.

**Trying rec BTN 3-bettor, we called from the CO out of position (9-6-4, flop checked through, K turn)** [2025-01-28 HHP]
- *Range:* "a lot of Ace King" (AA/KK/QQ/JJ would have c-bet the flop); AK never folds to one bet but folds to a check-raise and river jam. "Trying recs are some of the best players to bluff; they under-bluff and assume others do, too."
- *Size:* bluff (JT): check the turn, check-raise to about $1,500 over his $300, jam the river. Value (set): lead about $1,000 then jam or bet big ("leading minimizes fold equity, check-raising maximizes it"). The lead is ⚖4.
- *T-9-2, flop checked through, ace turn, he raises our small bet to $400:* A8, KQ, KJ, KT call small; a raise is weaker Ax (aces and tens aren't often in the range, sets removed). *Size:* bluff turn tiny ($100), call the raise, jam river blanks (Q, J, T, 3; not K or A). Value: lead the turn at least 2x pot; smaller than 2x is "a massive blunder."

**Trying rec BB, top card pairs on the river (22 for bottom set, 9 river)** [2025-01-28 HHP]
- *Range:* never folds a 9, might call an 8; after a wider flop line a lot of Adx, JT, Kx and Qx of hearts. *Size:* big early (flop $180, turn $750) because top pair rarely folds and we are deep; river jam. With a combo bluff: small flop ($40), bigger turn ($300, his range is wider), then give up or a tiny $150-200 river bluff.

**Reg BTN who range-bets $50 (Q-8-4, J-10-3)** [2025-01-28 HHP]

- *Size:* check-raise the flop to about $400 with a set and jam the river; with a bluff play the delayed line (check-call the flop, check-raise the turn to $725 over $150, jam). - *Reg CO cold-caller of a 3-bet (10-5-3):* the smaller you bet, the more they raise and stick around; pocket pairs fold to a big bet; 8x/9x/7x fold to a K turn unless you bet about 15% pot.

#### [2025-01-21 HHP] Exploits from one or two showdowns (1/2 to 2/5)

#### [2025-01-07 HHP] Range and "what happens if" (2/5 and 5/10, mostly vs recs)

- *Range:* would keep betting the turn with flush, straight or set. On a river check he bets both air (JhTh, QsTs) and thin value (88, 77, T9s, J9s); vs a bet he calls thin value and folds air.
- *Size:* check the whole range on a monotone connected board (aggressive players make betting mistakes); call his small flop bet; check the river to induce, since betting only gets thin value called.

**Rec BB on J-7-6 (5/10), J turn pairs the board** [2025-01-07 HHP]

#### [2024-12-10 HHP] The 9 biggest mistakes (1/2-2/5, about 200bb)
**Rec BB, river bluff with third pair or 7-high after a scary river (10-5-4, K turn, 10 river; 76dd on J-8-4-3-7)** [2024-12-10 HHP]
- *Range:* trips never fold; the rest is A-high/Q-high/J-high flush draws and QJ (autofold "if we breathe on this pot"); Jx (QJ, JT, J9) top pair "shriveled up"; straights and flushes snap it off.
- *Size:* don't give up because the card is bad. Bluff very small vs the draws, or near pot (175-200) to fold Jx (a bet that gets Jx to call is 110-125, under pot, so a near-pot bet folds it). Test: "if he flipped QJ/JT/J9, what size gets a call?" If the answer is under pot, bet bigger.

**Passive rec BB who check-raised the flop and checks after the board pairs (turn 10) or after a flush card (turn 2d)** [2024-12-10 HHP]
- *Paired turn:* value (Q10, 44, 1010: two pair, sets, boats) that checks now; check-raises with greed, or calls and donks the river. *Size:* small turn (60-80 into about $260), then tiny river (a quarter to a fifth of pot); big bets run into boats and a river check-raise jam.
- *Flush turn, he checks:* never a flush ("once in 6 million years", a check-raising flop player would bet it); he lays down two pair and sets to a big bet plus a river jam because the obvious draw arrived and we are deep. *Size:* 150 into about $185, keep a jam behind, jam if he checks. "The one spot in live poker where two pair and sets get folded", and "98% of the time you're lighting money on fire" elsewhere. ⚖6.

**Rec on a nut-changing turn (Q-7-6 then a 6)** [2024-12-10 HHP]
- *Range:* raises flushes and trips ("greed"); junk draws call the turn and fold the river; big bets make trips and small flushes just call and trap. *Size:* 30-35 (not 70 into 85) with value and with bluffs. Same on A3ss with a turned nut flush: small, so Qx, junk draws and flushes all put in money.

**Rec BB who called a small flop bet on a wet Q92 board, blank turn (we hold KJ)** [2024-12-10 HHP]
- *Range:* top pair (QJ/AQ/KQ) and A-high flush draws or JT keep calling a much bigger bet; weak stuff folds either way. *Size:* overbet the blank turn (about 100-120, not the 60 of a 2/3-3/4 autopilot), because the turn is where he is most inelastic. Agrees with the [2025-07-15] and [2025-07-01 HHP] blank-turn entries above.

**Rec in position who checks back on A-10-4 (we are OOP, CO opener)** [2024-12-10 HHP]
- *Range:* set, two pair and strong Ax bet when checked to; he checks back weak Ax, 10x and A4/A6/AQ ("he has no strong hands"), and snaps off A4/A6 after bet-bet-bet OOP. *Size:* don't triple-barrel into an in-position rec; range-check OOP and bluff Q-high when he checks back. His bet size telegraphs.

**Rec BB, turn with no equity (87ss, blank turn); TPTK vs a reg; thin-value regs** [2024-12-10 HHP]
- *Rec:* calls too wide preflop and on the flop and raises strong hands, so his turn range is weak and crippled: double-barrel with air, even with no equity ("equity is our get-out-of-jail-free card", the less we have the more fold equity we need). If you won't, check back more air on the flop.
- *Thinking reg BTN who checks back on A-10-6 (we hold QJ as the BB):* checks back showdown value (weak Ax, 10x, 99, KK/QQ/JJ), bets both strong hands and air. *Size:* check the turn again, then "big boy" check-raise. Betting into his check-back range gets snapped off by A3s/KK.
- *Reg in a 4-bet pot (A-10-9, we hold AA out of position vs a 3-bettor):* AK, AQ and all draws bet a turn check; KK/QQ/JJ call a small flop bet and fold to a big one. *Size:* 10-12% flop, check the turn, stack off when he bets ("regs make the most mistakes in lines with checks"). Same as the current 4-bet-pot A-high entry, [06-09] (postflop section 2).
- *Squeezing thinking reg:* linear ("good, better, best"). *Size:* vs a deep aggressive pro flat AA to induce the squeeze and bet the flop very small in a dry side pot.

**Bluff-happy reg vs tight nit reg** [2024-12-10 HHP]
- *Range:* the bluff-happy reg doesn't fold; the nit's bet "has it and believes you." *Size:* go super thin for value vs the bluff-happy pro, and "bluff the tight nit a lot." Decide by what the pool does, not by what you'd do. ⚖8.

#### [2024-10-08 HHP] Viewer-comment misconceptions (mostly $5/$10 straddle games, some 1/2 and 5/5)
**SB caller who leads a river into our set (UTG opener, 55 on J-6-5-9-3)** [2024-10-08 HHP]
- *Range:* flushes, Jx (AJs, KJ), occasional 78, flush and pair-plus-straight draws; essentially no air (only AhJ or KhJ at best). He calls a big turn bet ($200) with a wide range and won't put in more. *Size:* fold the set on the river (calling loses about $75-86 even giving him AhJ and half of J9). "If a player massively under-bluffs, every bluff catcher is better as a fold." Ignore what the pool thinks of the fold.

"Take 'the solver would bet small' and throw it out the window" (solvers assume equilibrium opponents). His numbers are from his own solver run under his assumed ranges.
