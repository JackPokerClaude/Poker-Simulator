## 7. Bluff-catching and river raises
**Marc's hero-call checklist [07-28]**
1. Do we beat value?
2. Is he capable of bluffing? *(Only "5, 10, 15%" of the pool can't. "Even fish are capable.")*
3. Did we give rope? (We bet small, checked back, or checked out of position as the PFR.)
4. Is the size significant in DOLLARS for this game? (About $400 at 2/5, $800 at 5/10.)
5. Does he have non-showdown value (missed draws)?
- **Bonus:** is a raise better than a call?
- If 1 and 2 are both no, just fold.
- **The same checklist, earlier version [2025-09-30, *90% of Money Is Won on the River*].** Marc's on-screen list is the same five questions plus the bonus, and he reuses five of the same hands (85cc facing $85 at 2/5: call; KQdd, a big check-raise then a river jam: fold; the 400bb 3-bet-pot bluff-raise; the AKhh 800-900 test; T8 on the ace river). "Pause, take a deep breath." Bluff-catching is about HIS range, not your hand's strength, so you'll call some weak hands and fold some strong ones. If 1 and 2 are both no (a super nit), just fold even the third or fourth nuts and skip the rest.
  - **Give-rope test, 85cc example [2025-09-30]:** BTN opens, we call flop and turn small, the board pairs on the river, we check and he bets $85. We don't beat value, he's capable, we gave rope, $85 at 2/5 isn't significant, and he has non-showdown value (busted draws): call. Vs "quite a lot of players" we have the best hand a lot.
  - **A KQdd fold [2025-09-30]:** 300bb, a sticky player who never folds Qx. We check-raised the flop big, check the turn when the ace comes, and he jams the river: relatively easy fold with two pair (we gave NO rope, $900+ is significant, and his only non-showdown value is 56).
- **Even earlier, the same checklist [2025-02-25 HHP, *Want to Lose at Poker*] (supporting, agrees).** "Don't ask 'should I call?'" Ask: do we beat any value, is he capable, did we give rope, is the size significant, does he have non-showdown value. Worked hand (no call/fold given): we open AJ, the BTN 3-bets, we call OOP, top pair on an A-high flop, check-call flop and turn, the river pairs the board, we check, he bets. At equilibrium a 3-bettor triple-barrels QJ/QT/Q9/K9/KT and diamond combos, but live he "probably isn't finding these bluffs," so he is under-bluffing. With a solver ask which hands are supposed to bluff, whether those bluffs are intuitive, and how thin he's supposed to go; on an A-high board in a 3-bet pot the bluffs are unintuitive and live players skip them. The bluff-catch questions are not the hand's strength, and "losing players demand answers, great players ask better questions."
- **Bluff-catching a REG's triple barrel, worked hands [2025-03-04 HHP, 2025-03-11 HHP, 2025-02-25 HHP].** Regs over-bluff wide configs, double-broadway boards and SRPs "where we give them rope"; they under-bluff tight configs, A-high boards and 4-bet pots. CALL: AJ in the BB vs a solid BTN reg on K-J-4 (flop 20 called, turn pot called, river check, he bets 180 into 242; his 180 isn't all the money or "all that scary," and he has natural bluffs QT, T9, AT) [2025-03-04 HHP]; A6hh in the BB vs a reg's river triple barrel after a turn overbet [2025-03-11 HHP]; Q-T-4 in a double-broadway 3-bet pot vs a reg's blank-river triple barrel ("get quite sticky"; he range c-bets AK/AJ that should have checked back) [2025-03-11 HHP]; K6dd (middle pair) in the BB vs a BTN reg's flop c-bet, big turn and Q river ("call it off with a pair of sixes") [2025-02-25 HHP]. FOLD: QQ vs a reg's river jam in a 4-bet pot; top pair vs an A-high triple barrel even in a wide config (CO opens, BTN reg 3-bets, A-8-5, river 4) because regs don't find the unintuitive bluffs on A-high boards [2025-03-11 HHP]; top pair vs a REC's big 3-bet-pot river jam on an A-high board (a rec, not a reg: under-bluffs) [2025-03-04 HHP].

- **Fold the set to an under-bluffer's river lead [2024-10-08 HHP, *Everything They Told You About Poker Is a LIE*] (supporting, agrees).** 55 (a set) on J-6-5-9-3: we open UTG, the SB caller check-calls a half-pot flop and a big $200 turn, then leads $350 into $590. His range is flushes, Jx and 9x for value with almost no bluffs (maybe AhJ). Calling loses money (his figures: about -$86 and -$75 even giving him the bluffs) because it only works if he turns top pair (KJ, QJ, JT) into a bluff often. "If a player massively under-bluffs, every bluff catcher is better as a fold than a call." "You don't have to call"; ignore what the pool thinks of the fold.
- **Read a villain from one or two showdowns, four examples [2025-01-21 HHP, *Exploit Any Poker Player Instantly*].** (1) A river size-splitter (a small half-pot bet after a turn check-check = bluffs like QJhh, A3hh; a big bet just under pot = value, and he shows AK/AQ/KQ that checked back): call the small bet with any showdown value (77-99, AK, AQ), "massively overfold" the big one, even 77/99 or the top of your range. Check back showdown-value hands on the turn so his river is easy to read. (2) A river OVERFOLDER (he folded A6 to a jam, solver reference: it should call 50-65%): jam every no-showdown-value hand (AQhh, AJhh, A4hh), "even with bad blockers." Even a slight overfold means "take every hand with no showdown value and pile in money." (3) A fast-player who raises sets and just calls with semi-bluffs (the coach's solver screen has 55 on 6-5-3 check-raising about 60% and AhQ about 34-35%, but this player fast-plays value nearly always and under-bluffs): his check-raise is value, so fold bluff-catchers (99, TT), stack him with top set, and after a flop check-call his range is weak, so your bluffs print on turns and rivers (be careful on flush/straight-completing turns, his passive draws hit more). (4) An early-position opener's third barrel on an A-high or triple-Broadway board (J9s/JT/66/55 are the hands that must bluff and he lacks them): overfold even top pair and float turns wider, because his river will be face up. The solver numbers are the coach's screen references, not verified.

- **Rec's turn raise of your tiny bet in a 3-bet pot, we call [2025-01-28 HHP, *This Poker Strategy Feels ILLEGAL*].** CO opens, a trying-rec BTN 3-bets and we call, the flop 10-9-2 checks through, the turn is an ace and we bet a tiny $100 (87s bluff, or 99 for value); he raises to $400. Sets are gone (he'd have bet the flop), Aces and Tens "aren't often in this range," so weaker Ax and hands like KQ, KJ, KT raise. The plan is to call, then jam river blanks (Q, J, T, 3, not K or A) to target Kx; if the river is a 6 (we hit), bet about $800 so an Ace calls. *(⚖ vs 01-20 point 4 above, "massively under-bluffed": a different spot, a 3-bet pot where the flop checked through and the turn is an ace; 01-20 point 4 is a raise in position on a brick turn. Both stand.)*
- **A tight rec's river check-raise after your small bet [2024-12-17 HHP, *What High Stakes Pros Know That Low Stakes Players Don't*].** KQo on K-Q-7 with hearts, a fish and an OMC (old tight rec) call the flop, the turn (a flush card) is checked back, we bet small on the river (about 250) and the OMC check-raises. He can't have a boat ("almost never") and OMCs "pride themselves on being able to fold anything but the nuts," so shove over the raise as a bluff to fold his ace-high and king-high flush: "call is the worst, then fold, so shove." *(Compare [2025-10-28]: the jackpot OMC gets a small river BLUFF, in `playbook-villains-oldest-videos.md`. A different action in a different spot: 2025-10-28 is our bluff into an OMC, this is our bluff-raise after his river check-raise. Both stand.)*
- **Pool under-triple-barrels, earliest version [2024-12-31 HHP, *The Truth About Low Stakes in 2025*].** As the caller "massively over-fold rivers": the pool under-triple-barrels bluffs by "3, 4, 5, maybe 10x," and a player who shows his bluffs is under-bluffing (he shows them so you think he can, then has it the next 11 times; Marc himself never shows bluffs and over-bluffs). Same read as [06-16] and [2025-06-03 HHP].

**Current answer, how big is "significant" in dollars [07-28].** About $400 at 2/5 and $800 at 5/10. *Superseded:* [2025-09-30] about $800 at 2/5 and $2K at 5/10. The rest of the checklist is the same.

**7 spots where the low-stakes pool bluffs more than it used to [01-20]** (the older, shorter version of 07-28's list; run the checklist instead of snap-folding):
1. **Stiff nut-flush-blocker (NFB) bluffs**, even from recs: river jams after your triple barrel, turn 3x check-raises. BUT ask how he got there with the NFB. After your big flop check-raise and two barrels, his NFB hands are sets and two pair. Fold, even the second nuts. NFB bluffs come from hands with no showdown value.
2. **Size clues:** a 3x raise, or a bet that doubles his last one, leans bluff. Bluffers act fast and don't think about size; value hands think 30-45 seconds. One clue, "not the end all be all." *(⚖ vs 04-14 below.)*
3. **A small in-position triple barrel = thin value** (KQ, AQ, weaker queens; never sets, two pair or straights with that size). "A bluff raise just prints."
4. **The flop checks through, you bet the turn, he raises in position:** still "massively under-bluffed" on a brick turn. On a wet turn (lots of new draws), especially with a 3x raise, you can start picking them off.
5. **A pairing turn** cuts his value combos while his draws keep firing. Float a rec's wet-board flop check-raise and bluff-catch the pairing-turn barrel. *(The 88 paired-T river: ⚖ below.)*
6. **Light in-position 3-bets and polar squeezes** (preflop sections 7 and 14).
7. **SPR under 2 in 3-bet and 4-bet pots:** players make BETTING mistakes and jam middling hands (77, AT, QT) because they don't want a turn or river decision. Not in deep SRPs or limped pots. *(Compare 08-18's "calling mistakes are more common than betting mistakes," which is about an SRP donk spot. Different spots, both stand.)*
- **Plus:** a big turn DONK after he called the flop on a wet board "doesn't make sense for value" (sets and two pair raise the flop or check-raise the turn). Get sticky and keep your options. *(⚖ vs the donk-size reads, below.)*

**Current answer, the 88 paired-T river jam [07-28].** 88 calls a BTN 3-bet, calls the c-bet and the double barrel when the turn pairs the T, then faces a river jam. Only "consider" the call vs the right player: it's a spot where "less and less of the pool will bluff." *Superseded:* [2026-01-20] "Tx or nothing," "super easy" to over-bluff.

- **Context, a turn LEAD in a 3-bet pot vs a small SRP donk [2024-10-08 HHP], not placed in either column.** 55 on the button, four limpers and the SB isolates to $30, we 3-bet to $140 and he calls, we A-Q-2 small and he calls, then he leads $230 on a 2c turn: fold. In a 3-bet pot on an ace-high board he puts you on AK, you have shown two aggressive actions and $230 is big for the game, so the lead is strong. The same lead is WEAK only in a small SRP, wide vs wide, a half-pot donk (his example: BTN opens 84, BB calls, A-Q-2, the BB leads $40). "Context decides": pot type, preflop and flop aggression, board and size. Sits next to the 08-04 and 06-23 "strong" column; not a ruling.

**River donks on nut-changing cards, and what a check means [2024-11-12 HHP, *Why Bluffing Is SO GOOD at Low Stakes (The HIDDEN Reason)*].** Recs raise their strong hands on the flop and on nut-changing turns, and often DONK strong hands on nut-changing rivers (the top card pairs, an obvious flush or straight completes). Example: BTN 3-bets the CO's open (200bb), the CO calls, Q-9-3, we range-bet 50 into 147, turn 8 (we bet small again), the river 2s completes the flush and he leads 250.
- **He donks, and we showed only strength (3-bet, small c-bet, small turn bet):** it is almost never a bluff. He has flushes (and trips/boats on a paired river). Fold about 80-87% of your range (the coach's number, from his own node-lock, not a live stat) and raise only hands that beat his flushes or real strong hands (K10+ and sets on the paired-ten river, all-in). "He has told us three times he has no strong hands," but the donk says otherwise. Agrees with [2025-06-03 HHP] #4 and [08-11, 08-04, 06-16] above.
- **He CHECKS that river:** his range is crippled (he raised or donked his good hands). Value bet thin and small, about 125 into 400 (30% pot), even with strong hands like the nut flush, because JJ/TT/9x call it. With NO showdown value (KTdd, AJo, AKo, A9s, K9s) overbet-jam about 2x pot (800): the coach's adjusted number is that JT/99/9x fold about 75% of the time (the solver says 62%).
- **How your turn size changes his river [2024-11-12].** A small turn bet makes him donk the river more; a bigger turn size makes him donk less (he thinks you'll bet thin); a blank river makes him trap more. On nut-changing turns size both thin value and low-equity bluffs DOWN.
- **When you donk the river yourself (OOP):** strong hands on nut-changing rivers, only vs fish who won't bet thin and massively under-bluff. Not vs thinking players or regs.

**Where the pool bluffs [07-28]:** when you look weak; when his range is wide and full of missed draws (BTN open vs your BB defend); stuck or tilted players; BTN/CO light 3-bets at the $5 level; a lead that doubles his last bet after the nuts changed; a stiff nut-flush-blocker river raise on a line that makes no sense; the top card pairing; spaz jams in big low-SPR pots. The clues stack. A quick 3x river check-raise alone isn't enough; you need a second clue.

- **Two more river-decision items from the older videos [2025-08-19]:** (1) Marc's question list for a hand with no answer given (AQ, we called a BTN 3-bet OOP, top pair, check-call flop and turn, check the river, he jams): do we beat value? Can he bluff here? Did we give him rope? Is the size significant for the game? Does he have many no-showdown-value hands? Better questions improve every future spot like it. (2) The "disrespectful fold": Garrett Adelstein at 200/400 folded KQ to a 33.5K river jam getting a great price (the jammer had a full house). Marc's point (after Art Papazian): fold even at a great price when he only jams value, don't call to avoid looking like a nit. "Play for money, not for looking good."

**Where fish bluff, Marc's on-screen list [2025-09-30]:** more when we look weak (we checked a street, just called, or bet small); more heads-up (multiway pots are "massively" under-bluffed); in smaller, single-raised pots; with smaller river sizes; more on the flop and turn than the river; with wide ranges (limped pots, BTN or CO vs BB); from late position; with brick draws; and when they're losing or tilting ("very, very emotional"). Many players who can make a small bluff can't make a big one. He doesn't rank them beyond that, and it overlaps the 07-28 list above.

**Where the pool bluffs too little or too much, Marc's solver screens [2025-12-16]:** the under-bluffed and over-bluffed spots are in section 1 of `playbook-postflop.md`. Under-bluffed: a river check-raise after your triple barrel, a turn check-raise vs your small top-pair bet. Over-bluffed: a wide-config straddle who bets 2x on a board-pairing river. Matches 07-28, 06-16, 01-20 and 04-14 on the first two.

- **Gethen's numbers [04-28]:** facing a pot-size bet, he figures villain "is supposed to be finding 1/3 of his range to be bluffs"; facing half pot, "only 25%." - **A trappy player's half-pot river bet after you gave rope: "Don't ever consider folding" [04-28].** Trappers get greedy once they finally bet, so half pot isn't their value size. A bluff-raise "is winning," but Gethen just calls vs an unknown in case it's K9.

**4-bet pot rivers [06-30]**
- **An aggressive BTN 4-bettor's river jam after the turn checks through:** it's air (A5, A6, Kxs). Thin value doesn't jam. Call with AK-high.
- **A cold 4-bet pot vs a pro, TT, turn checks through:** bet about 1/3 pot as a bluff. It folds JJ/KK without getting snapped by AK. If you hit a set, jam.
- **A-high 4-bet pot, turn checked through, our river sizes [undated HHP, *The Bet Sizing Mistake That's Costing You BIG*]:** value tiny (AA, to string KK-JJ along), bluff about 200-225 (to fold KK/QQ/JJ), bigger vs a passive player who may have checked back AQ/AK or an aggressive one with A4s-A5s. Small river bluffs and small river value from the same video (capped inelastic BB, elastic CO, passive vs aggressive) are also in postflop section 2.

**Where the pool does NOT bluff**
- A big river bet into 3 players after the draw came in [08-11].
- A donk on a flush-completing river after you c-bet and overbet: "fish getting greedy" [08-11, 08-04, 06-16]. Even a set is a bluff catcher there [06-16].
- A flop check-back, turn call, then a river raise: "one of the most under-bluffed lines in poker" [06-16].
- A triple barrel after your big flop check-raise [07-28].
- River bets over 2/3 pot [09-01], and big dollar sizes in general [07-28].
- A blocker doesn't matter if you don't beat value [08-11].
- **Thin value in position: check back the turn** [07-28]. Missed draws bluff the river for you, better hands bet bigger, and if he checks, you bet.
- **3-bet pot in position, small c-bet, turn checked back, he bets the river: call [2025-04-08 HHP].** He floats too wide for a small size, raised his strong hands (99, 44, Q9s) on the flop, and turning AK into a bluff means he has lots of bluffs. It's the 3-bet-pot version of the "best line in all of poker" above (2025-06-03 HHP #7).
- **Where the pool under- and over-bluffs, the earliest version [2025-04-08 HHP].** Under-bluffed: turn/river check-raises after you showed strength, big pots and big sizes, 3-bet and 4-bet pots, PFR triple barrels, ace-high boards. **Where the pool bluffs too little or too much: Marc's list of 8 and 8 [2025-06-03 HHP, *Where Poker Players BLUFF Too Much... And Where They NEVER Bluff*; the coach isn't named on the audio; the "solver" numbers are the coach's own references].** After you show aggression and he fights back, it's value. After you show weakness, he over-bluffs.
- **UNDER-bluffed (fold or give credit):**
  1. **PFR check-back on the flop, then a turn raise** ("massively under-bluffed": aggressive air would have bet the flop, and fear pushes the traps in now). Fold JJ-type hands.
  2. **IP small (about 1/3 pot) river triple barrel.** IP he doesn't fear embarrassment (you show first), so busted hands just check back. Overfold, or turn showdown value into a bluff. Calling is probably the least profitable. OOP, small river sizes are bluff-heavy.
  3. **An A-high triple barrel** (A-8-6, river J): he bets AK/AQ/AT thin (solvers check them back) and doesn't find the unintuitive bluffs (6x, K7hh, K5dd).
  4. **A big river donk after you showed only aggression** (capped but inelastic turn, river 6c, $250 into $400): flushes. Fold. A small (25-33%) donk is Kx showdown value, "still probably under-bluffed." If he shows a bluff once in 27 times, "they want you to call the next 25."
  5. **A multiway flop donk, and a multiway donk in a 3-bet pot** (JJ/QQ/KK "all the time"): overfold, TT included. With AA/KK pile it in.
  6. **A turn or river check-raise after you showed only aggression** (KJ, 77, 44 "all the time"; a river check-raise after raise, c-bet, overbet turn and river bet is 56/65 the nuts): "we can easily throw aces into the muck."
  7. **A small river stab after it checks through multiway** (AK, two callers, the BB stabs $35): fold every time. Bluffers would have bluffed the turn ("carte blanche").
  8. **A pot-size flop c-bet:** overfold, even an overpair to the board (55) or second pair. He throws "rock" 35-40% too often, so throw "paper." "Sometimes the best exploit is just disrespectfully folding."
- **OVER-bluffed (call or attack):**
  1. **A heads-up SMALL donk** ($20 into $52): call with value AND bluffs (76, QT). Don't raise, you don't want the garbage to fold. (The "weak top pair, middle pair, draws" range is survivorship bias.)
  2. **A wide-vs-wide c-bet in 3-bet pots (65dd, A-Q-8, he c-bets about 40% and range-bets nearly 100%):** check-raise pretty much any two cards without showdown value, even a small 3x. He says the button should c-bet this board "somewhere between 35 to 45%."
  3. **An IP small turn stab after you checked back the flop** (ace-high board): call linear (9x, 8x, 7x, even 66 beat K-high/J-high), and continue vs a river half pot.
  4. **OOP flop stabs when you check your range** (77, Q-6-4): check the entire range OOP to most live players and call the stab with showdown value. His size telegraphs his hand.
  5. **Ace-x turn as the PFR:** he double barrels too much, "play fifth street chicken" (call the turn, he gives up the river a lot).
  6. **Blank turns OOP in SRPs with lots of SPR:** check twice with value AND bluffs, then check-raise (5-high, for example), because checking twice gets money from his showdown value and air.
  7. **"The best line in all of poker":** c-bet small on the flop, check back the turn with showdown value, bluff-catch the river. The BB over-defends, raises strong hands and calls weak ones on the flop, and his strong hands bet big and greedy (75-100) on the river.
  8. **Small vs big flop c-bet ranges:** a small c-bet range is "a little bit too weak" (he throws paper 40% too often): call every time with showdown value, and check-raise non-showdown hands with a small size on wet boards. A big one is too strong: overfold. Static boards (A72) telegraph less, so don't call light or check-raise nearly as much there.

- **Top-of-range calls and jams [2025-06-24 HHP].** "It doesn't matter that we are top of range if our opponent is only jamming this river with the nut flush": a passive player rarely jams bluffs or worse value after a strong line (KJss vs a flush jam is +$1,285 EV in theory but -$38 if he never jams 99, QTs or J9s and jams smaller flushes only half the time). Your own river bluff vs a player who fast-plays: if his sets and two pair raised earlier, AK jams 100% (the solver says 40% when sets are still in his range), and QJhh jams 2x pot when 88/T8s fast-play and A8 folds (about +$68 vs checking). Vs a sticky player who calls JJ/TT/99, QTs on a brick river jams for value (the theory check-back assumes he folds those); vs a fold-happy one, try the small 250 bet.
- **Fold the second nuts to a jam after you showed max aggression [2025-06-10 HHP].** 400bb, a combo-draw check-raise on the flop, a big turn, a flush on the river: bet half pot (you can't jam for value vs overpairs/sets) and FOLD to his jam. He under-bluffs, so every bluff catcher is more profitable as a fold: "this is not a bad beat, this is a situation where we should just be saving money." The same fold applies to trips vs a river jam (aggressive player, 44/77/AQ "nine value combos," you hold a blocker to boats): "who says you can't fold this? Is someone at the table handcuffing you?" It agrees with 07-28's checklist and deep-stacks section 5; 07-28's calls depend on the specific spot.

## 9. Adjust by player type
**"Be water" [06-23]:**
- He under-bluffs: over-fold.
- He over-calls: under-bluff.
- He over-bluffs: over-call.
- **[2025-04-08 HHP] adds the folding side:** he over-folds (wide configs vs capped top pair, huge bets for the game's size, when up on the day): bluff. He over-calls (early v early 3-bet pots, small bets, uncapped ranges, when stuck or tilted): go huge for thin value and cut bluffs. "If they're 10% off, adjust 100%." Sizes are relative to the game: "$30 into a $25 game is a small size." The rare strong player gets softer exploits.
- Vs fish, fast-play strong hands; slowplaying gains nothing.
- Most of the win rate comes from fish, not from beating regs. Regs under-bluff tight configs, A-high boards and 4-bet pots, and over-bluff wide configs, double-broadway boards and SRPs. *(2025-08-19 repeats this list, and adds that fish under-bluff big 3-bet-pot stabs and barrels: overfold, and fast-play sets vs a fish PFR's big c-bet. Fish don't punish range bets either, so ask where he'll make the most mistakes.)* Fast-play where they under-bluff, trap where they over-bluff.
  - **The earliest worked version, Marc's four reg exploits [2025-03-11 HHP, *Why "Good" Poker Players Actually Suck*; 2025-02-25 HHP repeats the list] (supporting, agrees with the list above):** (1) FAST-PLAY where they under-bluff and hold a strong range: an early-vs-early 3-bet pot, we open 88, flop 8-6-3, he c-bets $40: check-raise 5x and pile in (he's "supposed to" fold JJ/TT/99 without a spade, and doesn't). (2) UNDER-BLUFF (give up) where they're supposed to fold strong hands: A5dd, tight config, river brick: don't bluff into JJ/TT/99. (3) TRAP where they over-bluff and have a wide range: Q-T-4, CO vs BTN 3-bet pot, TT set: check, let him keep the initiative (KJ, J9, AK, AJ triple-barrel too much). (4) OVER-BLUFF where they must call weak hands: 97cc, wet flop he raised his sets on, river 4 pairing the turn: jam about 2.5x (paired-river ⚖ CONFLICT in section 6 of `playbook-postflop-weakness-and-position.md`). The coach's point (2025-03-11): "on boards good for them, winning players make the same mistakes as fish," and regs under-bluff tight configs (LJ vs HJ 3-bet pot, 4-bet pots, A-high boards) and over-bluff wide ones (BTN vs BB, CO vs BTN 3-bet, double-broadway boards, SRPs).
  - **Beat fish first [2025-02-25 HHP].** Fish exploit number one: overfold a fish's huge c-bet, especially in 3-bet pots (66 in the CO vs a fish BTN 3-bettor, T-4-2, c-bet 130 into 157: fold; fold the double barrel even with "a shred of equity"; with a SET check-raise right away). Fish under-double- and under-triple-barrel bluffs and don't bet thin for value. Master this before the "shiny" reg exploits; the reg mistakes are smaller and "way less reliable." Also game-mindset section 6.
- **Two kinds of low-stakes regs [2024-12-10 HHP, *The 9 Biggest Mistakes Low Stakes Poker Pros Make*].** The "thin value" player under-bluffs (the tight nit whose bet means he has it and who believes you): bluff him a lot. The "big boy bluffs" player never goes thin and stations too much (he thinks everyone plays like him): go super thin for value. Decide by what the pool does, not by what you'd do yourself: over-bluffers call too much and under-bluffers fold too much because each assumes everyone plays like him. Also, pros checking their whole range out of position in SRPs either check-raise far too little or far too often, so pull them into unfamiliar lines: "these regs become fish out of their comfort zone." *(The examples, like flatting AKo early to let a fish in, are in `playbook-preflop.md`.)*
- **Trying recs are some of the best players to bluff [2025-01-28 HHP, *This Poker Strategy Feels ILLEGAL*].** "They under-bluff and assume others do, too." The example: a trying-rec 3-bettor holds "a lot of Ace King" and never folds it to a bet, so the bluff is a turn CHECK-RAISE (then a river jam), which folds AK; the value hand leads instead (details in `playbook-postflop-weakness-and-position.md`). He's also honest with timing (section 11). His flushes usually raise a small bet and slow down vs a big one ("check-call shell").
- **A river overfolder [2025-01-21 HHP] fits "he over-folds: bluff" above:** jam every no-showdown-value hand vs a player who folds A6 to a shove, even with bad blockers (section 7).
- **Fish who can't fold top pair: flip the river [2025-02-04 HHP, *Ask THESE 3 Questions to Make $100/hr*].** Most live players in 2025 won't stack off with top pair on the river in a normal SRP (elastic, so bluff big, value smaller). Vs a fish who never folds it (inelastic), flip it: value 2.5x pot, bluff small just to fold ace-high and king-high draws. Full flowchart: `playbook-postflop.md`.
- **Ignore "this board is good for my range" [06-23].** Middle set on A72: vs a passive player, bet, since he won't bet his Ax for you. Vs a stabber, check-raise.

Tight players tend to be callers, because they reach the river with a strong range. Loose players fold more rivers as a share of their range. ## 10. Strong hands
- **AA in a 3-bet pot on a dry, static board:** bet, bet, bet [08-25, 08-11].
- **Don't fear the one cooler** [08-25]. **Pull the trigger for thin value** in big pots [08-25].
- **Multiway on a wet board, bottom set, overbet plus a call:** get it all in now. Multiway is the one spot players trap even on wet boards [08-04].
- **Facing a check-raise from next to act with a player behind:** sets and two pair. With a nut draw, call if you have the odds [09-15].
- **Pot odds don't matter if he's never bluffing** [08-11]. The Broadway straight facing a passive BB's check-raise jam on a flush board: "where are the bluffs coming from?"
- **Defend tighter in the BB multiway** [08-25].

## 11. Live tells [08-11, 07-28, 08-18]
- **He reacts as you're about to act** (grabs chips, starts to flip his cards): he wants the opposite. A defensive chip-grab means "don't bet." If he half-lifts his cards after betting, he doesn't want a call. Happy players sit still [08-11].
- **A snap-call means medium strength, not a monster.** With a monster he'd at least pause to think about raising. Size down on the river [08-11].
- **A fast turn bet that doubles his flop bet** is weak-leaning; weak players act fast so they don't look weak [08-18]. A lead that doubles his last bet after the nuts changed is bluffy [07-28].
- **A quick 3x river check-raise** leans bluff, but never act on it alone [07-28]. [01-20]: bluffers pick the standard 3x or double the last bet fast; value hands take 30-45 seconds to pick a size.
- **Tells are only a tiebreaker [02-10]:** use one only on a 50/50 or 60/40 decision, and only once you know his baseline. Betting patterns beat tells.

**More live tells, Marc's "40+ tells" video [2025-09-16, Marc likely; he never names himself on audio].** Marc calls tells a win-rate multiplier on top of a system, not the foundation. He says each is "not definitive" or "wouldn't take these as gospel," so stack clues. The player-by-player reads (buy-in, chips, table talk) are in `playbook-villains-oldest-videos.md`.
- **Look at your own cards early ("play offense")** so you can spend the hand watching him. Acting strong means weak, acting weak means strong. A defensive move means he doesn't want the action you're about to take.
- **Quick preflop peel** = polar (monster or trash), especially before a 3-bet. **A long peel or suit double-check** = the middle of his range. **He looks away or down right after the turn/river** = the card likely helped. **He stares at the board** = neutral at best.
- **Snap-call on the flop or turn** = the middle of his range: cut the top and the bottom. **Snap-bet when the nuts change** (he bet the flop first, the turn brings a flush and a 4-card straight) = polar, because a medium hand would pause. Call a lot, raise little. With the nuts raise "absolutely massive" (his value never folds and his air folds anyway). With a bricked river hand, min-click 2-2.2x.
- **River, he tanks 15-30 seconds, then checks** = weak. Bluff big ("empty the clip"), size value down.
- **You grab chips to bet and he defensively grabs chips ("gold")** = "almost never" the top of his range, "99%" a very weak hand and likely a fold. Value: bet "even smaller than you think." Bluff: go even bigger, since there's no brick wall to run into and a bluff 15% too small is "infinitely more expensive" than one 150% too big when he folds 99%. **You count out chips to call and he lifts his cards to flip them** = most likely a missed draw or nothing: bluff-catch lighter, raise value small so he can spaz.
- **How he calls:** an aggressive chip-toss toward you = weak trying to look strong. A discreet call with the chips placed away = could be very strong.

**Current answer, how much to trust a tell [02-10].** A tell is only a tiebreaker: use one only on a 50/50 or 60/40 call, and only once you know his baseline. Betting patterns beat tells ("his ear wiggled" doesn't outweigh a check-raise, overbet, jam line). *Superseded:* [2025-09-16] "gold," "99%," no baseline required. Both say to stack clues. *Same advice, older, on record:* [2024-10-08 HHP, *Everything They Told You About Poker Is a LIE*] "tell reads without a baseline are worthless": you must see what his ear does when he value-bets and when he bluffs (his 97cc river jam vs a talkative rec's small bet was made on the line, "a strong hand would have bet the turn and sized bigger," not on the talking).

**Timing tells vs a rec [2025-01-28 HHP, *This Poker Strategy Feels ILLEGAL*] (supporting).** "Live players are honest with timing." A snap check on the river = Tx or weak, "close to zero trap" (remove A8/A5): bet small (about $120-140) so Tx calls. A tank check = more polarized, either give-up hands or hands that nearly bet: size up ($150-160). Vs a reg who calls our turn check-raise: a fast call = weaker (just fire the river); a tank = a hand that may have considered raising (bluff only on a diamond). ## 12. How Marc says to practice
- **Two kinds of mistakes [08-11, 08-25, 09-15]:**
  - **Logic:** your action contradicts the range you just gave him. Spot it by saying your read out loud; if the bet doesn't fit, that's the leak. Fix: before each size, ask "what's his range, and what does this bet do to it?"
  - **Calibration:** the logic is sound, but your read of this player or pool is off. Fix: more hours on him, attention, and a "dossier" (tag stickies, one-size bettors). "Very, very easy to fix."
  - Don't treat each mistake as a one-off. Diagnose which type it was.
- **Flop checklist:** pot type, villain, SPR, texture, your hand class, default action, then "any reason to deviate?" [08-25]
- **Drill** with a hand trainer on a 30-second shot clock, reasoning out loud [08-25, 08-04]. "If we make the practice harder than it actually is," you'll crush at the table.
- **Study the solver for levers, not answers [2025-12-16].** Don't use it to "scratch a curiosity itch" about your exact hand. Find where the pool bluffs too little or too much, change one frequency (how often he folds top pair), and see where the play flips. Marc also once limited himself to two sizes in a session (10% pot or 2x pot) as an exercise. His method is in section 1 of `playbook-postflop.md`.
- **Ranging drill [07-07]:** deal BTN vs BB hands and a flop from a real deck, then name his range after a check-call vs a check-raise, at 100bb vs 200bb, and by turn type. Ranging is "the #1 skill for $100/hr." No solver until you're at about $100/hr.
- **Review whole sessions,** including small pots and winning sessions. Write down your in-the-moment reasoning [09-15].
- **Process advice from Marc's review of his student Tommy [2025-08-05].** Judge the process, not the result. Find what you missed in your thinking (Tommy's example: as the caller he only considered call or fold, never raise). Set ONE leak goal per session ("find more spots as a caller," "what do I do when they're capped on the turn"). Vlogging kept him accountable. Most people get sad about mistakes, and Tommy turns each one into a fight to learn. The "stop reviewing hands" title means review the reasoning, not the hand.
- **Better questions, not answers [2025-08-19]:** for a hand with no answer given, Marc lists five questions instead (do we beat value, can he bluff, did we give rope, is the size significant, does he have natural bluffs), so every future spot like it improves. Copying strategy snippets without the "why" punts EV: always start from the villain's range. **Think in repetitions:** "which play makes the most money over the next 100 times?" [08-25]
- **Scared money:** watch for it when tired or late in a session [08-25].
- **Calibration comes with reps; the money is in carrying the logic [06-23].** "It's not about the calibration. If players do X, we do Y" [06-30].
- **Stuck on a spot? Ask better questions, not for the answer [06-23]:** Do we beat value? Can he bluff this line? Did we give rope? Is the bet big in dollars for this game? Does he have natural bluffs?
- **Two questions every hand, then plan the whole hand [2025-01-07 HHP, *This Secret Turns Poker Players Into CRUSHERS*].** (1) What is his range? (2) What happens if (we bet, we check, and each response)? Play the branches before you bet, so the turn and river sizes are decided on the flop.
- **The repeatable thought process, with timed drills [undated HHP: start-over].** Every hand: (1) What is his range? (2) What is my default? (3) What happens if (bigger / check back / raise / fold)? On rivers add: what would I do with the inverse (the bluff size sets the value size and the reverse)? Drill it on a clock: ranging at about 30 seconds a street, then the thought-process drill at 45, then 30, then 15 seconds a street. Sizes and worked hands are in postflop section 2 (IP defaults block).
- **Stay logic-consistent [2024-10-08 HHP].** If he calls a river jam with 77, then jam your value (88, T9s, TT) thin and stop jamming bluffs; if he folds 77, jam the bluffs. You can't bet 500 as a bluff and jam for value on the same read. Size off his range, not off fear of being "unbalanced": "we're not trying to be balanced against people who can't spell balance." A weak or capped villain gets big bluffs and smaller value; a strong, two-pair-heavy one gets jam value and small bluffs at his downgraded hands. Don't ask "am I balanced," ask what your two cards want to do: bluffs and value are played differently [2025-01-28 HHP].
- **Use the calculator, not gut feel [2024-10-08 HHP].** Outs x4 (flop) or x2 (turn) is a shortcut, not exact, and blocker effects on equity are usually too small to matter. (Also the project rule: equity comes from the poker-oracle tools.)
- **Most live players won't adjust to you [2024-10-08 HHP];** a strategy more complex than tying your shoe won't be adjusted to by 99% of the pool. Vs a very strong pro or reg, dial the exploits back and play a fundamentals baseline. Take every +EV spot and let variance sort itself out (bankroll: `playbook-game-mindset.md`).
- **Don't worry about being exploited [06-23].** Marc told a solver his exact (over-bluffing) strategy and asked it to exploit him. The answer was "call," which 99% of players wouldn't find. Spend the effort exploiting THEM.
