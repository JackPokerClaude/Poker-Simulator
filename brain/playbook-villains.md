## Postflop villain cheat sheet: his range, how he reacts, and the coach's size
Only what the coach said. Sizes are at the stakes of the example hand (the [04-14] sizes are from Marc's 10/20-100/200 footage). Last merged 2026-09-29 (batch 9 added the section "Reads from the [2026-04-21 HHP] multiway quiz" near the end; the four other batch 9 videos are 2025 videos, so their reads are in `playbook-villains-oldest-videos.md`).

**Over-stabber** [08-25, 09-15]
- *Range after he repeats a small bet:* 8x, 77, 4x, gutshots, air.
- *vs a big bet (your raise):* folds almost everything. Pair-plus-draw calls the turn, then folds a brick river to a jam.
- *Size:* check-raise draws. Turn: raise big (about 200 at 2/5), then jam a brick river.

**Trappy fish / rarely check-raises** [08-25]
- *Range:* just calls with strong hands. His check-raises lean bluff.
- *Size:* c-bet half pot, and more often. Call or re-raise his check-raise with draws.

**Whale** [08-25]
- *Range:* Qx, weak pairs, Tx, draws.
- *vs a big bet:* inelastic. 800-900 may not fold AT or T9.
- *Size:* flop 200-250 at 10/20, turn overbet (800+). If he'll stab, check.

**Fish (general)** [08-25]
- *Range:* more weak Ax than you'd think, and Q9 that checks to call. Few sets on static boards. Fast-plays flushes on the flop.
- *vs a small bet:* raises strong hands, calls weak ones.
- *vs a big bet:* inelastic on the turn. With no brick wall, a river jam folds him.
- *Size:* big turn. River: jam as a bluff, or about 350 for value vs 8x.

**Fish on a Q-Q-x board** [08-25]
- *vs a small turn bet:* 88 and 5x call; the Qx that flatted raises.
- *Size:* iso $60 over an early limp. Flop a bit under half pot, tiny turn, river value 110-135.

**Tight player** [08-25]
- *Range:* strong is 4x, boats, some overpairs. Medium is 6x, 77-22.
- *Size:* turn 35-45. Small pairs call it and fold to big bets.

**Iso player who called your 3-bet, turn checked through** [08-25]
- *Range:* Jx (AJ, QJ, JTs), 88-TT. More inelastic on the river; he believes you less.
- *Size:* about 200 to get called by 88-TT, or overbet (about 400) to target Jx.

**Small river leader after the turn checks through** [08-25] *(⚖ vs [08-04], postflop section 4)*
- *Range:* one-pair Ax, best about AT/AJ. Two pair bets bigger.
- *Size:* raise to about 300.

**Rec BB who leads small on a 4-flush / 4-straight river** [08-04] *(⚖ vs [08-25], postflop section 4)*
- *Range:* polar: the 9d or better, the Kd, Qd, straight flush, or bluffs (75, TJ). Two pair and sets check to call.
- *vs a raise:* Kd/Qd never fold; the Jd might.
- *Size:* no raise. Call with showdown value.

**Rec BB defender after you c-bet and overbet the turn** [08-04, 08-11]
- *Flush-completing river, he checks:* uncapped. Recs check flushes expecting a third barrel. Qx/9x are elastic. Bluff just under pot.
- *He donks the flush river instead:* "simply a flush." Fold, even with the nut blocker.

**UTG limp-caller** [09-15]
- *Range:* lots of Ax and small pairs. Check-calls sets and two pair.
- *vs an overbet:* folds up to about AJ/AQ.
- *Size:* overbet-or-check. Ace turn after his half-pot stab: check-raise small (150 over 50), then jam the river.

**Old tight rec ("OMC")** [09-15]
- *Range:* potting the flop = QQ/AA. After calling small bets on a queen turn, he never has a queen.
- *Size:* check-raise trips, small river vs KK/QQ. Bluff 400 into 110 when capped.

**Older sticky rec** [09-15]
- *Range:* lots of 97s, T9s, 7x, 77, 8x, A8. *vs a big bet:* 80 doesn't fold them.
- *Size:* about 80 with JJ, not 45.

**Sticky "limp everything" rec** [09-15]
- *Range:* A-high, 77, draws. Won't fold A-high even to 300.
- *Size:* with a set, flop 50-100 into 110, turn the biggest size a draw calls, river put it in.

**Super sticky player** [08-11]
- *vs a big bet:* calls 2.5x pot with second pair.
- *Size:* no big bluffs. Go "unbelievably large" for thin value.

**Passive calling station** [09-15]
- *vs a check:* checks back. *vs a big bet:* 88 puts you on AK and calls.
- *Size:* low SPR, bet the flop, jam the turn.

**Rec who donks his flushes** [09-15]
- *If he checks a flush-completing turn:* "very capped."
- *Size:* 60-70 into 80 on the turn, jam the river unless a spade comes.

**Rec who checks the river to call** [09-15, 08-11]
- *Range:* A7, 7x, 88, missed draws. After double-barreling, a too-strong range with Qx.
- *Size:* $100 into $60 gets called by A7. With trips after his double barrel, 450-500.

**Rec who triple-barrels the river** [07-28, 08-11]
- *Wide range, you gave rope, small dollars ($85-120 at 2/5):* lots of missed draws. Call a bluff catcher.
- *An ace river, polar* (AQ, AK, two pair, trips, straights, or air): jam value, call or fold bluff catchers. ⚖ Air: postflop section 7.
- *After your big flop check-raise:* under-bluffed. Consider folding.

**In-position player who stabs small every street** [07-28]
- *Range:* linear thin value (KQ, AQ). Strong hands size up.
- *Size:* raise your bluff catcher as a bluff.

**BB who check-raises your river bet quickly to 3x** [07-28]
- *Range:* straights, sets and two pair would have raised the flop; Kx bets the river. Busted draws are left.
- *Size:* "on the fence." Get sticky if you looked weak. If you bet three streets, fold.

**Aggressive "spaz" in big low-SPR pots** [07-28]
- *Range:* merged (JJ, middle pair). "They couldn't even tell you" if it's value.
- *Size:* call, or jam over the top. "I love to punish."

**Player who stabs a wet board at 90% pot** [08-11]
- *Range:* sets, 99, TT, strong draws. Few bluffs.
- *Size:* check-raise your set.

**Player who snap-calls your overbet** [08-11]
- *Range:* medium strength (top or middle pair, draws), not monsters.
- *Size:* go smaller on the river.

**Caller who raises strong hands on wet flops** [08-11]
- *After a small c-bet call:* capped (99, 22, J9s would have raised). Jx is left.
- *Size:* play a big pot on a blank turn. Don't go small again.

**Uncapped next-to-act caller, multiway** [08-04, 09-15]
- *Range:* can trap sets (55, 44) and 78s "to let the players behind in."
- *vs a medium bet (200-250):* raises his sets, squeezing out the other player.
- *Size:* vs inelastic callers, pot to 1.2x so he just calls and brings the other player along.

**Next-to-act caller in a squeeze pot** [07-28]
- *He calls the flop next to act, then raises your river value bet with the stiff nut-flush blocker:* "doesn't make a whole lot of sense." Get sticky and call.

**BB defender after a pot-size c-bet** [08-11]
- *Range:* "very, very strong" (AK, AQ, A8). He keeps betting.
- *Size:* check the turn and let him bet.

**Limped pot, multiway recs** [09-15]
- *Range:* "very, very few" strong combos. After the turn checks through, capped at Tx, A7, 88, 99.
- *vs a small bet:* $40 gets snapped by 99. *vs a big bet:* Qx gets squeamish; stickier on a paired river.
- *Size:* bluff the river 100+ (125-150). With a queen for value, about pot (about 50).

**Wide limp-caller when everyone looks weak** [09-15]
- *vs a check:* if you checked back the flop and the other player checked the turn, he stabs any two.
- *Size:* call with any showdown value (even 33).

**Loose cold-callers in a 4-way 3-bet pot** [09-15]
- *Range:* suited broadways, pairs, Tx, draws. Everything calls a jam at SPR about 1.
- *Size:* c-bet, then jam the turn.

**Semi-strong player who called next to act, player behind** [09-15]
- *Range:* uncapped; his turn check-raise is sets and two pair.
- *Size:* small turn. If he called LAST to act (capped), overbet.

**Wide CO opener c-betting into your BB** [09-15]
- *Range:* overpairs, sets, bluffs. *Size:* just call thin top pair.

**Rec donker ("Debbie Donks")** [06-23]
- *Range:* sets check-raise. Top pair/overpair donks about pot. A half-pot donk is weak and marginal. Draws check-call.
- *vs a raise after her small turn donk:* mucks fast.
- *Size:* raise her big donk large ($300, not $150). Flat her half-pot donk, even with a set. After donk small, donk small, then a check: river value about $150-175.

**Limper who donks multiway** [06-23]
- *Range:* a multiway donk = top pair or better. When he checks instead: "insanely capped."
- *Size:* range-bet, and if he check-calls, keep barreling.

**BB who calls a river jam with second pair** [06-23]
- *Size:* with top pair next time, overbet the turn about 2x pot and get it all in.

**Flop trapper on wet boards** [06-16]
- *Range:* flats his sets, so on blank turns he's uncapped. His wet-flop check-raise is capped (few sets or two pair).
- *Size:* blank turn small. Vs his check-raise, 3-bet the flop and keep the big turn overbet behind (or flat and bet big when he checks the turn).

**Multiway c-bettor who put a boat in the big size** [06-16]
- *Big c-bet:* overfold, even top pair. *Small c-bet:* too weak; attack with draws and backdoors.

**Flush-draw maniac** (check-raises and stacks off with naked draws) [06-16, 06-23]
- *Just calls, then the flush comes in:* no flushes, capped.
- *Size:* bet-3-bet thin vs his check-raise. Go big on the flush turn, then pile in.

**Player who checks back the nuts on the flop** [06-16]
- *After a flop check-back:* uncapped. *When he c-bets:* capped.
- *Size:* smaller turn check-raise after his check-back and no stack-off. Big check-raise vs his c-bet on wet boards.

**Under-triple-barreler** (gave up with 7-high) [06-16]
- *Size:* call flop and turn wider. Fold bluff catchers (AT) to his triple barrel.

**Over-triple-barreler** (triple-barreled AKdd) [06-16]
- *His river check:* showdown value (TT, A9, 88, 77). Bluff-catch rivers a bit wider. Consider turning 66 into a bluff when he checks.

**Middle-range flop check-back** [06-16]
- *Range:* 44, AK-high, TT, 9x. Not sets or top pair, not air. He wants an easy call-call.
- *Size:* make him play for stacks. The exact line is on screen only [unclear].

**Passive player** (c-bets only value and big draws, drops thin value on the turn) [06-30]
- *River triple barrel:* value only. Fold where you'd call a normal player.

**Early-position opener when the flush comes in, you hold the K of the suit** [06-30]
- *Range:* few flushes (QJs, 98s of the suit), mostly thick value (QQ, AQ, AT).
- *vs a big bet:* a typical 2/5 player folds two pair and even sets to check-raise turn, overbet-jam river.
- *Size:* the biggest turn check-raise that still leaves a river overbet, then jam. Vs someone who never folds two pair: don't. Vs a BTN opener (lots of flushes): fold.

**Check-raiser whose flush-completing turn gets checked** (300bb) [06-30]
- *Range:* no flushes (the pool check-raises naked draws only about 8-15%, Marc's number).
- *Size:* the biggest turn bet J9 still calls, then a river overbet jam. If the turn PAIRS instead: small turn, very small river.

**Cold caller of your 3-bet (AA, CO vs BTN 3-bet, BB cold-calls)** [06-30]
- *Range:* thick value (TT, JJ, QQ), which won't fold to about 2/3 pot on the river.
- *Size:* size up the turn to set up the river, then get it in.

**LAG who bets small on the river after the turn checks through (3-bet pot)** [06-09]
- *Range:* AK, AA, KQs, hands that "barely" beat top pair. Flushes and sets bet the turn.
- *Size:* check-raise all-in.

### The four player types [02-17] (Marc, *Exploit Any Player After a Couple Hands*, 2026-02-17)
Readable after one or two showdowns. Rows: passive / aggressive. Columns: caller (calls too much) / folder (overfolds).
- **Passive clues:** very neat chip stacks, waits for the BB to post, complains the game is too tight, a rules nit. At showdown: flats TT/JJ/AK preflop, checks back clear thin value, gives up 6-high/7-high on the river.
- **Aggressive clues:** messy piles, plays lots of pots, posts or straddles instead of waiting, "I usually play bigger." At showdown: 3-bets suited connectors (weak clue), thin river value, keeps betting two pair when the draw comes in, turns second or third pair into a bluff (the strongest clue).
- **Caller clues:** a "non-believer" who pays off the table nit. Tight preflop players tend to be callers.
- **Folder clues:** "sees monsters under the bed," flashes top pair and mucks it. Loose players fold more rivers as a share of their range (though plenty are still stations).

**Passive caller** [02-17]
- *Range:* tighter than normal, even on the BTN; lots of overpairs behind a big c-bet in a 3-bet pot. Won't value-bet thin or bluff enough.
- *vs a small bet:* folds A-high/K-high. Trips won't raise and bricked draws won't spaz.
- *vs a big bet:* sticky. Won't fold top pair or an overpair in a 3-bet pot. "Just because they're not raising doesn't mean they're ever folding."
- *Size:* fast-play sets (a small flop check-raise, then jam the turn). Bluff small, only for A/K-high. With the nuts, river 2.5x pot. Fold TT to his big 3-bet-pot c-bet.

**Passive folder** [02-17]
- *Range:* folds most on the RIVER, once aces, straights or flushes can come. Double-barrels draws, then gives them up on the river. His check-raises have few draws (J9, 22, AJ).
- *vs a big bet:* a lone river blast after you called twice gets called by JJ/AT. Two big signals (a turn check-raise, then the river) fold Tx/JJ. Deep, even J9/22 or an A-high flush folds to a jam.
- *Size:* still fast-play value (he folds least early in the hand). "Tell him twice." Float the flop and turn wider. Never show a bluff; say "good fold."

**Aggro caller** [02-17, 01-27]
- *Range:* wide light 3-bets (KQs, A5s, ATs, KJs, QJs, 88) that won't fold to a 4-bet. Value-bets thin and keeps bluffing if given rope. Bluffs a lot in wide vs wide spots.
- *vs a small river bet:* trips put in more money; busted draws may check-raise and spaz.
- *vs a big bet:* his turn hands are inelastic. On the river trips call and busted draws fold.
- *Size:* 4-bet linear. Nuts on the turn: 2-2.5x pot, then river "spaz bait" (tiny). Give rope with two pair. Hero-call good hands.

**Aggro folder** [02-17]: Marc's own style ("the pool tends to under bluff"). Rare and the toughest. No exploits given (he says they're in the paid course).

### Reads from the other older Marc videos (merged 2026-09-29)

**Unknown player, first hand** [02-10]
- *Assume:* he under-bluffs and won't punish you. Pull back only once he proves otherwise.

**Player who calls down with every pair** [02-10]
- *Size:* no bluffs. Thin value.

**Known trapper** [02-10] *(agrees with the ↔ blank-turn column, postflop section 9)*
- *Range:* slowplays strong hands on the flop, so he isn't capped.
- *Size:* c-bet small so he raises; don't overbet a dry blank turn.

**3-bettor who closed the action** [04-14]
- *Range:* under-3-bet, "probably massively." After a flop and turn check-through: "a whole lot of showdown value," few bluffs.
- *vs a check:* checks back showdown value, bets river value. Small flop bet, then a turn double barrel = strong.
- *Size:* call JJ preflop, fold it to the double barrel. Fold 99 to his river bet after check-check.

**Rec who double-barrels big** [04-14]
- *Range:* strong (a queen, KQ). Won't fold a queen.
- *Size:* with trips, check-raise the turn.

**Thinking rec who snap-raises your small river block bet 3x** [04-14] *(⚖ vs [01-20], postflop section 7)*
- *Range:* AA/KK/AJ that checked the turn to keep the pot small. No boats or straights.
- *Size:* a modest 3-bet bluff, not a jam. It folds his overpairs.

**Sticky but aware station** [04-14]
- *vs a small bet on a nut-changing turn:* raises flushes, calls weak Kx.
- *vs a big bet:* Kx folds. Qx and trips never fold on a paired-top-card river.
- *Size:* small turn, then a river bet to fold Kx.

**Over-c-bettor who floats way too wide** [04-14]
- *vs a check (turn):* overbets. *Size:* call; give rope and pay off the flush.

**Wide blind completers, big pot** [04-14]
- *Range after two calls:* Ax (A8, A7, A4), pocket pairs, suited connectors. Almost never JT.
- *Size:* bluff 17K, bigger than the about-pot value size (8-9K at 25/50), to fold Ax.

**Very aggressive check-raiser who bluffs when given rope** [04-14]
- *vs a raise on the river:* a bet-3-bet jam is always a flush. Fold the J-high flush.

**Sticky, super-aggressive "non-believer"** [04-14]
- *vs a big bet:* A10-type hands won't fold to a reasonable raise. *Size:* value-raise two pair, maybe bigger.

**In-position small river bettor** [01-27: 3-bet pot, turn checked through; 01-20: small triple barrel]
- *Range:* thin value (AK, KQ, KJ). Under-bluffed: in position a missed draw just mucks.
- *Size:* raise showdown value (ATs) big as a bluff. A small in-position triple barrel: bluff-raise it ("prints").

**Reg who range-c-bets a bad board** [01-27]
- *vs a check-raise:* should defend very wide, but continues too tight.
- *Size:* check-raise a lot. If called, shut down.

**BB defender vs your range bet** [01-27]
- *vs a small bet:* check-raises only the intuitive hands; won't find K7cc, A8cc, KJcc. So range-bet until he punishes it.

**In-position caller who called the c-bet and a double barrel** [01-27]
- *Range:* "a ton of two pair" (A10, 44, TT, A6, A2) plus plain Ax. In-position players slow-play much more.
- *Size:* don't triple-barrel. Cap him earlier.

**BB who check-raises your c-bet, then checks a pairing turn** [01-27]
- *Range:* full houses (44, QT) slowing down to trap, plus scared draws. "Bifurcated."
- *Size:* don't bluff big into it. "Scalpel instead of jackhammer."

**BB defender on a flush-and-pairing turn** [01-27]
- *vs a small bet:* raises flushes much more often. *vs a big bet:* slow-plays flushes; 6x just calls.
- *Size:* tiny turn, so his flushes identify themselves.

**Good player who checks back the flop** [01-27]
- *Range:* thin value and showdown value (Ax, Tx, 99, 44, maybe KK). Not sets, two pair or draws.
- *vs a small lead:* "click call call." *vs a big bet:* Ax calls even a river overbet.
- *Size:* check the turn, massive check-raise his stab. If it checks through, river about 3x pot.

**Rec with the stiff nut-flush blocker** [01-20]
- *Range:* A8o/AJ/AQ with the Ad as a bluff: river jams after your triple barrel, 3x turn check-raises.
- *When not:* after your flop check-raise and two barrels, his NFB hands are sets and two pair. Fold.

**Rec BB who check-raises a wet flop, then barrels a pairing turn** [01-20]
- *Range:* fewer value combos once the board pairs; "a bunch of his good draws" keep firing.
- *Size:* float the flop, bluff-catch the turn.

**Limper who calls your c-bet, then donks the turn big (wet board)** [01-20] *(⚖ vs the donk-size reads, postflop section 7)*
- *Range:* showdown value plus a draw (89, Q8s) and "some BS." 55/JT would raise the flop.
- *Size:* get sticky. Bluff-raise a small river bet; check back if he checks.

**Players at SPR under 2 in 3-bet and 4-bet pots** [01-20]
- *Behavior:* jam middling hands (77, AT, QT). "They wouldn't be able to tell you if it's bluff or value."

### Reads from the [04-28] guest video (2026-04-28), vs unknown 2/5 players, 200bb unless noted

**Average passive 2/5 fish, BB vs your BTN open, wet T-high flop** [04-28]
- *Range:* fast-plays sets and two pair on the flop, so by the turn he's capped: top pair (Tx) and flush draws. River: trips or missed draws.
- *vs a small bet:* 33/55-type hands call the flop, but you "can't get a big bet out of them."
- *vs a big bet:* top pair and draws are inelastic and call the turn. On the river, even 800 might not fold trips.
- *Size:* 25 flop, 150 turn with value AND bluffs. River: jam the full house. Bluff about 150 (the smallest size that folds missed draws). With showdown value (77), check back the turn and fold to his pot-size river bet.

**Unknown who calls your 3-bet (CO vs BTN) on a wet flop** [04-28]
- *Range:* turn: uncapped (A-high flushes, other flushes), plus Jx and weak pairs with a spade. If he doesn't raise the turn or donk the river, he's capped at about QQ.
- *vs a small bet:* flushes check-raise the turn or donk the river; weak hands call.
- *vs a big bet:* a river jam folds "a ton of Jx." 450-500 folds a jack.
- *vs a check:* bets half pot on the river with a "somewhat polarized," over-bluffed range.
- *Size:* flop 50-60, turn about 100. River value 350-400; river bluff: jam. With TT facing his half-pot river bet: call.

**Unknown BTN who 3-bet your CO open, then c-bets half pot (you're out of position)** [04-28]
- *Range:* stronger than it should be (the pool under-3-bets here). On the river: AK, QQ, AA, KQ. Under-bluffs.
- *vs a check:* checks back the turn a lot and won't bet thin for value on the river. Bets 150 on the river with a strong range.
- *vs a big bet:* calls a river lead and never raises. On a pairing river he gets "more stubborn and sticky."
- *Size:* flush: check the turn, lead the river 400-500. A5 bluff: jam the river. KJ vs his 150 river bet: check-raise jam. ⚖ Donking the turn: postflop section 5.

**Trappy "Helmuth" (price-sensitive, sees monsters)** [04-28]
- *Range:* a few more traps than normal, lots of 9x (even offsuit), draws like 97. River: a few two pairs, Kx of diamonds, missed draws. Over-bluffs his half-pot river bet.
- *vs a small bet:* calls; 97 may put money in.
- *vs a big bet:* price-sensitive, so it narrows his range. 9x calls 80-100 on the river but folds to about 150.
- *Size:* 25 flop, 40-50 turn, river value 80-100, river bluff 135-150. Call his half-pot river bet with A7. ↔ postflop section 9.

**Aggressive check-raiser at 300bb, you call and he checks the flush turn** [04-28] *(the same tell as Marc's 300bb check-raiser above [06-30]; see deep-stacks section 4)*
- *Range:* 99, 22, J9, AJ, KJ. He'd bet a turned flush, so the check means no flush. "Maybe one or two bluffs" on the river.
- *vs a big bet:* sets and two pair are inelastic and call about pot on the turn. AJ without the As may fold. "Getting people to fold two pairs is hard."
- *vs a check:* sets and two pair often bet the river.
- *Size:* flush: turn 450-500, then jam. Bluff (KQcc): turn about pot (300 max), keep $1,000+ behind, then jam. KK: check the turn, river 250-300.

**Unknown who calls your 4-bet, dry A-high board (you're out of position)** [04-28] *(collapsed in postflop section 2: the 06-09 numbers are the current answer)*
- *Range:* AK, AQ (heavily blocked; won't raise the flop), KK-TT ("18 to 24 combos"). No draws.
- *vs a check:* bets his Ax on the turn; doesn't protect his check-back range.
- *vs a small bet:* calls with almost everything.
- *vs a big bet:* only Ax calls a big flop bet. On the river, JJ starts folding at about 175-200.
- *Size:* flop 50-60, check the turn, river value 100-125, bluff 200-225.

### Reads from the [2026-04-21 HHP] multiway quiz (*I Made a 20 Question Multiway Quiz*, ~200bb, about 1/2-2/5 dollar sizes; the lessons are in postflop section 4)
Only what the coach said; the flops for several hands were not spoken. The same rules as [2025-08-12] in `playbook-villains-oldest-videos.md`; nothing here contradicts it except the worst-draw line (postflop section 4).

**Callers behind you on a wet, dynamic flop (players left to act after you check)** [2026-04-21 HHP]
- *vs a check:* very likely to stab, and the stabbed hands "shrivel up and fold by the river." *Size:* check, then a big check-raise (a set, 44 on A-Q-4); pile it in by the turn. Players stab less the more multiway, and slow-play strong hands on dry static boards.

**Players who fast-play on a wet board, one player behind you** [2026-04-21 HHP]
- *Range:* two pair, sets, monster draws (raise a small bet), thin draws stick around for a good price. *Size:* nut straight, small ($35 into ~$100). Dry static A-9-2 with one behind: big ($90), because they slow-play sets (22) and two pair (A2s).

**Players who fold AJ/AT/A8 by the river at ~200bb+** [2026-04-21 HHP]
- *Range on a dry A-high board:* 55, A5s and trapped AK (many won't 3-bet AK from the BB) are the strong hands; AJ/AT/A8 and sometimes AQ are the medium ones. *Size:* K5s bet-bet-jam (blocks 55, A5s, AK), big.

**Limping Larry (a rec who limps middling hands, small pairs, suited broadways) betting pot multiway** [2026-04-21 HHP]
- *Range:* TT+ (an EP raising rec is already too strong, then he pots it). With AK he "shuts it down," and when he stabs he goes smaller. *Size:* fold 88 overpair (facing $100 into a pot of about that).

**Aware pro c-betting into three players, or an EP raiser's c-bet plus a BTN call** [2026-04-21 HHP]
- *Range:* a "damn good hand" (or big draws like an A-high flush draw or T9hh); the next-to-act caller may trap with two pair or sets. *Size:* fold JJ (closer if last to act), fold 89cc top pair no kicker vs an LJ half-pot plus BTN call, fold A5s bottom pair vs a CO bet plus BTN call.

**Multiway donker** [2026-04-21 HHP]
- *Range:* two pair (63s), sets (33), vulnerable two pair, Jx (AJ, KJ), monster draws (45cc); little air (a heads-up donk is "full of [ __ ]" about 90%). Never folds a strong hand: "very inelastic." *Size:* continue with Jx or better, raise 66 to $300.
- *BB who overcalled the flop, then donks $250 into the turn (Q-7-6):* straights, two pair+, monster combos. Fold KK.

**Who is capped, after a check-through or a bet and a call** [2026-04-21 HHP]
- After the PFR checks through, the CO and BTN are capped (they bet strong hands once the PFR checks) and the LJ raiser is capped more than 75% of the time on wet boards. After a bet and a call, the bettor and the next-to-act caller are uncapped and the BB is capped (raises 55/KT). The only exception he names is a viewer who checks his whole range.
