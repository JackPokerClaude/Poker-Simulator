## 1. Pick the most profitable game, not the biggest
- **"I'm a 10/20 player" is a logic mistake [08-11].** Marc's story: a Commerce reg who "ran good at 5/10" bought in short at 10/20, lost for weeks and ended up at 2/3. Seven regs at 10/20 can be worse than a soft 2/5.
- **Toughest places for 2/5-5/10 [07-07]:** Vegas, LA, Miami, Dallas. Full of pros and travelling European pros, with little rec money outside events.
- **Best games [07-07]:** underlisted ones. Listed as 1/3 or 2/5 but actually bigger: match-the-stack, bomb pots, deep stacks. High-cost-of-living cities have better games but higher monthly expenses.
- **Rake matters [08-04]:** marginal flats (e.g. SB vs a HJ open) lose money in raked games but are OK in time-charge games.
- **Pools now vs 2016-17 [02-10]:** far fewer players who give it away at 2/5-5/10, and more YouTube-trained players who are "trying." The edges are still large but take more work.
  - **[2024-12-31 HHP] the same shift, from the 5/5 side:** in 2017 an SB 3-bet was QQ/JJ+ and maybe AK. Now the pool 3-bets 88/99/AJs/AQo, even T8s. It is more aggressive, not always more correct: open a little tighter from early position, but they still call too wide in the BB and under-4-bet (preflop and postflop specifics are in `playbook-preflop.md` and postflop section 7).
  - **His results, for scale [2024-12-31 HHP]:** 2017 5/5 sample about 1,000 hours at roughly 15bb/hr (~$65/hr). The 2024-25 challenge, about 300 hours: 18bb/hr, ~$109/hr with the jackpot counted. He says he doesn't know if $100/hr is sustainable, but six figures at low stakes is "very achievable" and $200-250k a year at 5/10 or 10/20 for a 40-hour-a-week pro. *Marc's own numbers, not verified.*
- **Moving up: keep the same process [04-14].** Marc's mistakes from his own high-stakes footage (Marc's, not Joan's):
  - **Letting run-bad affect you.** The fix is the same repeatable thought process you use at your normal stakes.
  - **Putting opponents on a pedestal.** Don't credit players with wizard-level thinking just because the game is bigger. Marc folded top-top thinking the raiser "has to know" his range is strong.
  - **Letting the money get to your head.** Deciding from "do I want to play for it all?" instead of from his range, or folding to an overbet you'd call at your normal stakes.
  - **Talking yourself out of bluffs in small pots, and out of thin value.** Details are in the postflop playbook.
- **Side games (7-2, stand-up, bounty) [02-10]:** Marc loves them because they reward players who adapt over "preflop charts in trench coats." **[2025-10-28]** he says to push side games vs a studied "GTO wizard" type ("drag him to the jungle"). A bounty example: winning 3 pots in a row makes everyone pay the winner $100-200, so a bounty player has a bigger "invisible pot" and isos any two [2025-12-16] (`playbook-preflop.md` section 4).
- **Seat selection vs a whale [2025-10-28]:** the "god seat" is on the whale's RIGHT, not his left. He's a VPIP 70-80%+ player who isos about 80% of the time.
  - **From his right:** limp knowing he'll iso, see what the table does, and squeeze big to get heads-up with him. Or get out of the way when there's a 3-bet and a 4-bet jam behind. You act "basically last," and the game gets good because of how the rest of the table reacts to him.
  - **From his left:** you get stuck in 4-way 3-bet pots or run into the UTG limp-trapper.
  - Marc almost never seat-changes. "Don't abuse this," don't make it obvious, and move before he sits down, not after. The whale's postflop reads are in `playbook-villains-oldest-videos.md`.

## 2. Straddles and bomb pots [07-07]
- **Straddle if you're a winning player.** Fewer bb/hr but more $/hr: 10bb/hr at 2/5 = $50/hr, 7bb/hr at 2/5/10 = $70/hr. "You don't pay rent with big blinds per hour."
  - Don't push it if the fish or a VIP is "adamantly opposed." Don't make the fish leave.
  - **[02-10] adds:** the worst players decide. Uneasy recs often won't complain, they just leave (maybe for good), so speak up for them. Push hard only when the VIP wants a bigger game and a grinding reg is the one objecting. Tap the brakes when the VIP is unhappy. "You can skin a sheep once and shear it many many times." (Same $50 vs $70 example as 07-07.)
- **Bomb pots:**
  - Judge RELATIVE hand strength. 7-8 players, PLO or double board raise the bar; an overpair or trips can be weak.
  - From early position, **check your whole range.** With strong hands, check-raise after someone bets. Don't put money in with medium or weak hands.
  - Leading from early position is the mistake.
- **Play the side games and bomb pots too.** What counts is the skill gap.
- **[2024-12-31 HHP] agrees with the straddle math above:** with the straddle on, the edge in bb shrinks (about 10bb/hr to 7-8) but $/hr rises (about $50 to $70-80). Side games (7-2, stand-up) and bomb pots raise the hourly win rate. His exceptions: very short stacks, or everyone at the table uncomfortable (same idea as not pushing it when the fish is opposed).
- **Even Marc calls a straddle-pot BTN open with any two "too light" [2025-12-16].** The spot: a straddle is on, the BTN opens any two to 40 vs 3 rec blinds. He says it's "too light" even with a skill edge and deep stacks, and live players may be light preflop too.
## 3. Be good for the game [07-07]
- "Be the casino." A gracious loser and a silent winner. Chatty when losing, quiet when winning.
- Say yes to straddles, stand-up games and seat swaps.
- Let recs choose run it once or twice (same EV). Vs pros, Marc runs it once. *(⚖ vs the tilter, below.)*
- Private games: give to get (bring players, be likeable). Marc avoids home games: cheating risk, not getting paid, rake skimming.
  - [02-10] adds: get invited by being someone people enjoy playing with; Gethen's skill was listening. Be authentic, and don't lie about playing professionally. Marc regrets saying he worked in "music." Gethen got cheated and went unpaid in private games and won't play them again [02-10].
- **Don't fast-roll recreational players, and don't table-change or seat-change to squeeze small edges [2025-04-08 HHP].** Recs know who the pros are, so long-term EV beats a quick edge (same idea as the [2025-10-28] whale-seat note above: "don't abuse this"). Be authentic about being a pro (matches [02-10] above); poker is work.
- **Table image is "way overrated" [02-10].** A lot of the table isn't paying attention, and you always have an image that's good for something (bluffing or value).

**Running it once [07-07, 2025-10-28] (merged: different villains, not a real conflict).** Let recs choose run it once or twice (same EV); vs pros, run it once [07-07]. Exception: vs the emotional tilter, "always, always, always" run it once when the game allows, because running it once or twice doesn't change equity, only variance, and we handle variance better than he does (a bad beat makes him punt) [2025-10-28].

**Showing bluffs [02-10, 02-17] (merged: the second is the special case of the first).** Almost never show a bluff: keep the "tight ABC, he has it" image. The exceptions are a big action VIP (not a nit), where a shown bluff builds friendly competition, and stream or private games, where showing sometimes gets you invited back [02-10]. Vs a passive FOLDER you're bluffing repeatedly, NEVER show: say "good fold" and show your value hands. Marc bluffed a 5/5 reg for 9-10 months this way ("my ego is [not] more important than making money") [02-17].

## 4. When to stay, when to leave [07-07]
- **Decide on process, not results.** Ask: am I on my A-game? Is the game still good? Am I tired? ([2025-04-08 HHP] same: "the sky isn't falling"; process over results. His own 600-700 hour breakeven stretch at 5/10 ended when he went back to pure exploit and worked about 70 hours a week.)
- **Up and deep in a soft game is your biggest edge. Stay.** (Same as deep-stacks section 1.)
- **Three strikes, for PROCESS mistakes only** (e.g. 4-betting a nit who "always has it," miscounting the SPR for a river bluff, 4-bet bluffing 75s into a whale who never folds [02-10]). Coolers and bluffs that got snapped off don't count.
  1. Strike 1: 5-minute break [07-07, 02-10].
  2. Strike 2: ⚖ see below.
  3. Strike 3: rack up, however good the game is [07-07, 02-10]. Marc: even with "seven Randall Emmetts" at the table. He's never been glad he stayed foggy.

## 5. Bankroll and variance [07-07, 08-04]
- **If poker isn't your income** and losing 3-4 buy-ins won't hurt your daily life, don't sweat bankroll [07-07]. Miguel follows the same view [08-04]. For non-pros, bankroll is "26th on the priority list," and skill matters more [06-23]. If poker is your income, it matters. Never play stakes that hurt your life.
- **If it's your income:** track 800-1,000+ hours, then run the numbers (Marc names Primedope). His conversions: bb/hr x 3 ≈ bb/100 (about 30-35 hands/hr); live standard deviation about 100-115 per hour x 3. *These are Marc's rough figures, not computed here.*
- **A higher win rate means fewer long downswings**, even though taking every +EV spot swings harder hour to hour. You can't reach 15bb+/hr playing tight ABC.
- **Taking every marginal +EV spot is not a bankroll risk [2024-12-24 HHP, 2024-10-08 HHP] (agrees with the [07-07] "higher win rate" bullet above, no conflict).** Spots where you risk a lot to win a little (a river jam-bluff, a thin call-down) still add roughly 3-6bb of EV each, about 3bb/hr if they come up in ~10% of hands. His data, with a math professor: self-described high-variance students won about 13bb/hr vs about 6bb/hr for tag-nit players, with a similar biggest downswing once win rate is counted, so "high variance is a myth." Live standard deviation is over 100bb per hour (about 200bb per 100 hands), far above online (matches the 100-115 above). [2024-10-08] the same point: a 15bb/hr "high-variance" crusher never had a losing 5,000-hand sample (about 166 hours), while an 8bb/hr low-variance player had losing samples over 15,000 hands (about 500 hours). "When I hear people say they want to reduce variance, I'm hearing I don't want to win all of the money." [2024-12-10] adds: you can't win money by avoiding regs, and a higher win rate means smaller downswings. *These are his figures, not computed here.*
- **Cash safety:** use a players bank or safe box. Carry $200-400 as "give-up" money.

## 6. Getting better [07-07, 08-04]
- **The one skill for $100/hr: ranging your opponent.** Drill it with a real deck (details in postflop section 12, in `playbook-postflop-bluffs-and-rivers.md`).
- **No solver until you're consistently at about $100/hr.** First: ranging, a repeatable thought process, and basic exploits vs your actual opponents.
  - **⚖ CONFLICT: Joan to decide. When to start solver / theory: [undated HHP: start-over] (undated) vs the line above ([07-07] / [08-04], "about $100/hr").** The undated video says don't go into the solver until your first six figures: low-stakes players deviate so far that equilibrium (a defensive strategy) won't win the most, so ranging, the thought process and the basics come first. It adds that theory helps a lot later ("learn the baseline so you can deviate harder"). The thresholds differ (first $100K earned vs about $100/hr). No winner.
- **The weekly plan [undated HHP: start-over].** Build a fixed weekly list and "complete it no matter what"; if the week was too hard ease back next week, if too easy add. Set goals you actually want and accept the cost: best-in-the-world means 80-90 hours a week, six figures does not. **Full-time (six figures in a year):** play 32 hours (Tue, Thu, Fri, Sat) and study 8: about 4 hours Monday of timed ranging drills (about 30 seconds a street, preflop through river) and a Wednesday timed thought-process drill (45 seconds a street, then 30, then 15). **Part-time (a car, vacations):** play 16 hours (Fri, Sat) and study 4 on Sunday (thought-process and ranging drills).
- **Fix ONE leak, not eight small ones [08-04].** Miguel's plan was one leak (sizing vs capped/uncapped ranges) and one filter.
- **Skip the shiny objects [06-23].** Beating regs is complex and low-yield. Beating fish is where the win rate comes from.
  - **[2025-02-25 HHP, *Want to Lose at Poker?*] (supporting, agrees; added 2026-09-29):** beat fish first: their mistakes are bigger and reliable, while regs' mistakes are smaller and "way less reliable." Do the boring work, skip pre-study routines and excuses ("how do I generate hands?"), and outwork a 9-to-5. "Losing players demand answers, great players ask better questions": before "should I call?" ask the bluff-catch questions (postflop section 7).
- **"What you avoid controls you" [06-23].** The part of your game you avoid working on is what keeps players stuck at 2/5 and below.
- **Preset strategies break [06-23].** The stable's 117-page node-lock guide failed in spots it didn't cover (multiway, 4-bet pots, weird donks). Set session goals vs the actual table instead ("play tight today," "don't fold today").
- **What separated the winners among the 700 players they coached [02-10]:** seeing things fast (the stable's test: name the gutshots on J72 in under 5 seconds), self-awareness, and brute work ethic. Fast talkers "try until it gets hard and then quit." Alex went from $25/hr at 2/5 to 10/20 and bigger by out-working the stable.
- **Work on what you're bad at,** not on getting 1% better at your strengths [02-10].
- **Practice thinking, not watching [02-10].** Replicate in-game decisions at home with a deck, record yourself, then review. Marc narrows it to two things: ranging and your repeatable "what happens if" process. Drill your weak spot (ranging, bluff-catching) 20 times an hour. It's OK to be exhausted after 45 minutes of asking these questions at the table.
- **HHP strategy is not "bluff big, value bet small" [02-10].** It's asking better questions and finding each opponent's mistakes. Add one tool at a time, over months. "When you give someone a strategy, you give them permission to stop thinking" [02-10].
- **Aim to be logically consistent, not right [02-10].** Folding to a check-raiser who barrels when his flush draws got there is logical. If he shows air, that's a calibration miss, which reps fix. "If being right is really important to you there are way better hobbies than poker" [02-10].
- **Don't project your tendencies [01-27].** Bluffers call because "I'd bluff," scared players fold because "I'd give up," stations don't bluff because "I'd call." Marc's own tendency is to overfold, so he forces himself to spend an extra 10 seconds on the bluff-catching checklist.
- **The two questions, every hand [2025-01-07 HHP]:** "what is their range?" and "what happens if?" (bet, check, and each response), planned before you bet. Same as the [02-10] repeatable process above.
- **Hand notes [02-10]:** shorthand in your phone's notes app, 9-20 seconds. Your thought process matters more than exact suits.
- **Should you go pro? "No," for 99% of people [07-07]:** no healthcare or 401k, and income drops if your skill stalls. A winner at 2/5 on 10-15 hrs/week is a reasonable goal. Make sure the goal matches the work you're willing to put in.

## 7. Reads before (and away from) the hands
- **Exploit unknowns from hand one [02-10].** Assume they under-bluff and won't punish you, then pull back if they prove otherwise. "Play defense until I know something" is a "massive punt"; assuming mistakes is more profitable "19 out of 20 times." Gethen used to wait hours for proof, and the player left 40 minutes later [02-10]. Respect is earned, not handed out "like Halloween candy." **[2025-04-08 HHP]** the same idea: "respect is expensive." Start with offense vs live players and pull back only once someone shows he's strong. **[2025-01-21 HHP]** shows you can build an exploit from just 1-2 showdowns (how he sizes his bluffs vs value, whether he fast-plays, whether he overfolds rivers), then chain it flop to river (details in the postflop playbooks and `playbook-villains-oldest-videos.md`). **[2024-10-08 HHP] "they will adjust to you":** most live players won't (99% of the pool won't adjust to anything more complex than tying a shoe). Vs a very strong pro or reg, dial the exploits back and play a fundamental baseline.
- **Don't decide your style before you see the table [2025-10-28].** "Tight today" or "aggressive today" is picked before you know who's there. "Play like water" and exploit the humans in front of you. Marc's nine live types, with how to spot each one (jackpot OMC, "GTO wizard," rules nit, tilter, monster-under-the-bed folder, ego player, donker, trapper, whale) and their exploits, are in `playbook-villains-oldest-videos.md`.
- **More buy-in and table reads [2025-09-16]** (Marc's experience-based stereotypes, "your mileage may vary"): always tops up to the max, pulls big chips from a pocket, or buys in with all $100 chips = likely a reg/pro, so dial exploits back. Buys in short, takes all $5 chips (10 stacks at 2/5), or buys in with $20 bills = likely a fish (the $20-bill buyer is more often a splashy action fish than a passive nit). Pros take a mix of chips. A significant other sitting behind him, or showing a losing hand for sympathy = fish. Waiting to post the BB after a break = tighter. Buying the button/BB instead of waiting = more action. Full list in `playbook-villains-oldest-videos.md`. *(The [02-10] buy-in reads below are the same idea; they're consistent, the 2025-09-16 list is longer.)*
- **Buy-in reads [02-10]:**
  - Buys in with a stack of $20 bills, or $450 in a $1k-cap game: rec.
  - Posts his first hand when he doesn't have to: impatient, likes to play hands, too wide.
  - Pulls a $5k chip from a backpack and asks for the chip runner by name: probably a pro.
- **Chip stacks and habits [02-17]:** the passive, aggressive, caller and folder clues are in `playbook-villains.md` (player types).
- **Table talk [02-10]:** how people treat money says how they treat poker. A guy bragging about a risky investment may overvalue a flush draw. A guy who finds index funds too risky ("cash in a sock under the mattress"): think about bluffing him on the river.
- **Live tells [02-10]:** only a tiebreaker on 50/50 or 60/40 decisions, and only once you know his baseline. Betting patterns beat tells ("his ear wiggled" doesn't outweigh a check-raise, overbet, jam line). **[2024-10-08 HHP]** the same baseline view: a tell means nothing without a baseline. Also postflop section 11 (in `playbook-postflop-bluffs-and-rivers.md`). ⚖ **[2025-09-16]** Marc's *40+ Poker Tells* calls tells a win-rate multiplier and the defensive chip-grab "gold," "99%," "really reliable," with no baseline requirement mentioned. The side-by-side table with 02-10 is in postflop section 11.
- **Process advice [2025-08-05, Marc reviewing his student Tommy]:** judge the process, not the result, and set one leak goal per session. Details in postflop section 12.
