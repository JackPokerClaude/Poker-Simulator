## 1. Read his range first
Do this before picking an action or a size.

**Marc's two steps, every street [06-30]:**
1. **"What's his range?"** Sort it into buckets, then cross buckets off as the hand goes. Ask "what can we eliminate?"
2. **"What happens if?"** For each option (fold, call, raise; or check, small, big), ask how each bucket responds.
- **Level 2 [06-16]:** once a showdown tells you where his strong hands go, ask "what does it mean when he does the OPPOSITE?" If his strong hands take line A, line B is capped. Humans "lean way too far one way or the other," so they're rarely balanced between two lines.
- **Carry the logic to the next hand [06-23].** He donks top pair multiway, so the time he checks, he's capped.
- **The showdown framework [2025-04-22 HHP, the earlier version of 06-16].** For each showdown: (1) what CAN we say, (2) what can we NOT say (don't over-conclude: "over-bluffing" doesn't mean no value, "capped" doesn't mean he folds), (3) the INVERSE, then three exploits.
- **Range in broad buckets, starting with what he DOESN'T have [2025-04-08 HHP].** BB calls the flop: no AA/KK/AKs, no bottom 25-40%. He calls a big turn: thin value and low-equity draws are gone, so the river is Jx plus busted high-equity draws. Carry it street to street without getting stuck on combos. On each hand ask: his range, the default size, what happens if I check or bet, and on rivers, what would I do with the inverse?
- **Range by elimination, timed drills [undated HHP: start-over].** Start with what he does NOT have, cut hands on every action, think in big buckets early, and carry the SAME range street to street: no new hands on the turn that you removed on the flop (no aces on the turn if he just called preflop). A rec BB defends about 40-50% of hands (HHP's number). Preflop he drops AA, KK, some QQ (he'd 3-bet); a flop call cuts a lot of 22, 99, J9 (they raise some of the time) and most air; a turn overbet call leaves mostly Jx, with 9x folding "maybe 75%", small flush draws "50, 60%" and open-enders "maybe 50%" (all HHP's rough numbers, not computed). In real hands: a flush on the turn raises "at a high percentage", so most flushes are cut and the river is "a ton of Jx and maybe open-enders with a spade." Practice with timed drills (about 30 seconds a street), see `playbook-game-mindset.md` section 6.
- **That's all the in-hand thinking you need [01-27]:** his range, then "what happens if I check / bet / raise?" On the river add "what would I do with the inverse?" (bluffing: what size would value use? value: what size would a bluff use?). Vs fish, drop MDF, blocker math, "my range" narration and "what if he check-raises." "Monkey clapping cymbals."
- **Marc's solver-study method [2025-12-16].** Marc's numbers come from his own solver screens in the video. Don't look up "call or fold?" for your exact hand. Look for the lever instead:
  1. Which of his hands are supposed to bluff here, and how much value reaches this spot?
  2. Does THIS villain have more or less value than that (does he fast-play sets on the flop?), and does he actually find those bluffs?
  3. Test "what if" by changing one frequency (how often he folds top pair, how often he checks trips) and see where the play flips from winning to losing. "The answer isn't what's important... the levers" are.
  - **Where the pool bluffs too little (fold bluff catchers):** a river check-raise after you triple-barrel with a river overbet; a turn check-raise vs your small top-pair bet (the pool finds AxJ/AxQ with the nut-flush blocker, not 76s/86s/64s); a big river bet after the flop checked through, IF he checks back a lot of Kx on dry K-high boards.
  - **Where the pool bluffs too much (call):** a wide-config player (a straddle) who leads the turn, then bets about 2x his last bet on a board-pairing river. Missed straight draws always bluff, flushes size up, trips sometimes check. Marc's nodelock: our fold drops from 43% at equilibrium to about 10%.
  - **The point isn't "they call TT 26%."** It's whether he calls or folds MORE than equilibrium.
- **Using solver output without hiding behind it [2025-06-24 HHP, *Smart Poker Players Keep Losing*; the sim numbers are the coach's own solver screens].** "Pure fold / pure check / mandatory call in theory" only holds if he plays the way the solver assumes, so ask what he is actually doing:
  - **Don't memorize frequencies.** See which bluffs and thin value he's supposed to have at equilibrium, then ask "is he doing that?" If not, adjust.
  - **Never "randomize" a close call** (J9s vs a triple barrel, about a coin flip at equilibrium). He bluffs off all his straight draws = call. He gives up or takes showdown with AK/KJ = fold. "In live poker we have so much information."
  - **Turn calls depend on his river bluffing.** A villain who gives up rivers (doesn't triple barrel) = call the turn much more (99 goes from a pure fold to calling 100%). One who fires the third barrel enough = fold more. Pay attention to showdowns.
  - **Great blockers alone don't make a call** (A9hh vs a river jam is a 96% call in theory): if he usually fires two barrels and gives up, "you don't even need the solver," fold.
  - **A nit's 3-bet changes the 4-bet bluff:** the solver 4-bet bluffs K9s about 69% only because the 3-bet range includes borderline hands (66, J9s, A8s). A nit who lacks them just has QQ and jams: "we lit $200 on fire." ([2025-04-08 HHP] same lesson: A3dd 4-bet bluff into a nit SB's jam lost $225; "GTO against how your opponent plays" is the max exploit.)
  - **"The solver would bet small" doesn't apply live [2024-10-08 HHP, *Everything They Told You About Poker Is a LIE*].** A9s on 9-8-3, turn 2s, BB checks: $160 beat $45 by a lot under the ranges he assigned (he continues about 81% vs $45 and folds about 45% vs $160: 8x, weaker 9x, gutshots without a heart). Solvers only put hands into the sizes you offer and assume the opponent plays equilibrium. The goal is the most money vs the actual player.
  - **"Board is better for him" is only half the question:** ask "will he check-raise enough?" On 654, if he skips the unintuitive check-raise bluffs, AA (checked 73% in theory) bets 100% and you range-bet. Vs a player who range-bets small, KQo check-raises (56% in his sim) on A54.

**Strong or weak? [08-18]** Stack the clues; none is certain on its own.
| Strong range | Weak range |
|---|---|
| Big c-bet (3/4 pot or pot; even half to 60% in a 3-bet pot) | Small c-bet on a wet, dynamic board, heads-up |
| Any multiway c-bet | Wide config (BTN vs BB, CO vs BTN 3-bet pot) |
| Tight config (EP vs EP, 3-bet or 4-bet pots) | A turn bet that doubles his flop bet, thrown out fast |
| Turn bet over 2/3 to 3/4 pot, or a big river bet | |
- A small c-bet on a **bone-dry static board is NOT weak**. Players bet small there with strong hands too, since no turn scares them. Treat it as strong until proven otherwise.
- **Multiway reads [06-30, 06-16, 06-23]:**
  - A pro c-bets into 3 players and a fish calls next to act: fold. The pro has no thin value or air. A caller with players behind is where even fish trap.
  - A fish betting into 3 players is weaker than a pro doing it. Call. If both players check the river, value-bet.
  - A big multiway c-bet is "too strong," so overfold, even top pair. A small multiway c-bet is too weak, so attack it with low-equity draws and backdoors.
  - A multiway donk is "much, much stronger" than a heads-up donk.
- **Tight configs have little air [06-30, 05-12].** Early vs early 3-bet pots and 4-bet pots have few bluffs and few flush combos.
  - Fold JJ to a triple barrel in an LJ vs HJ 3-bet pot.
  - Fold QQ to a double barrel in a 4-bet pot.
  - Fold QQ to a river bet over 60% pot in an EP vs EP 3-bet pot once the draws got there.
  - **[2025-03-11 HHP, 2025-02-25 HHP, 2025-03-04 HHP] agree (supporting):** vs a winning player in a tight config, overfold. 77 and AJcc fold to a 1/3 c-bet on 8-6-3 (LJ open, HJ 3-bet); 99 vs a pro's early 3-bet on 8-6-3: call the flop, fold the turn barrel; QQ folds to a river jam in a 4-bet pot (he doesn't 4-bet KJ, KT, K9, A4 or 78 enough, so he arrives short of bluffs); fold top pair to a rec's big river jam on an A-high board in a 3-bet pot (recs under-triple-barrel, so every bluff catcher is a fold, and he "massively" under-double-barrels). Reason (2025-03-11): in early vs early spots most of our range "has something going on," so those hands sit near the bottom.
- **"Learn the why, not the rule" [2025-02-25 HHP, *Want to Lose at Poker*].** "Don't c-bet boards bad for your range," "don't barrel without equity" and "big bluffs, small value" are memorized rules that are right at equilibrium but wrong vs live players who aren't at equilibrium. Two small villain mistakes (he fast-plays strong hands too much, he skips unintuitive check-raise bluffs) flip the whole strategy. His meta-point: the rules change when the pool changes (2016: thin bet-fold value; "maybe" 2025: bluff a lot and fold to big bets; if the pool starts over-bluffing and over-calling, the memorized rules stop working).
- **River range in buckets, then one logical thread [2024-11-26 HHP, 2024-12-31 HHP, 2024-10-08 HHP].** Sort his river range into weak stuff, strong stuff and missed draws. A bucket only counts if it is more than about 10% of his range. The sizes for each mix are in section 2. Then keep one thread: if he calls with 77, jam your value (88, T9s, TT) and stop bluffing; if he folds it, bluff. Don't bet 500 as a bluff and jam for value. Chain the exploit from one or two showdowns (a size split, a river overfold): the reads are in `playbook-villains-oldest-videos.md` (batch 12 section).
- **Is a lead weak? Context decides [2024-10-08 HHP].** A turn lead in a 3-bet pot on an ace-high board, after you showed two aggressive actions (3-bet, small c-bet), is strong: players put you on AK, and $230 is big for a 5/5 game (fold 55 to it). It is weak only in a small single-raised pot, wide vs wide, as a half-pot donk (BTN opens 84, BB calls, A-Q-2, he leads $40). Pot type, earlier aggression, board and size decide it.

**Capped or uncapped? Then elastic or inelastic? [08-04]** Miguel's two-question filter. Skip the board narration and go straight to these.
- **Capped** = his action already showed his strength: he checked, bet small into several players, or called where he'd raise a big hand.
- **Uncapped** = he can still have the nuts. Next-to-act callers multiway are uncapped, because that's where players trap.
- **Inelastic** hands don't fold to size: draws, pair plus draw, top pair that checked. **Elastic** hands fold once the size gets big.
- Sizing: see section 2.

**Does my hand have showdown value? [07-07]** Three steps:
1. **Configuration.** Wide ranges (BTN vs BB): A-high can have showdown value. Tight ranges (a cold 4-bettor bets the turn): QQ doesn't.
2. **Passive or aggressive?** Loose Larry's wide 3-bets leave A-high some value. Nitty Nedward's JJ+ may leave JJ none.
3. **Did his size telegraph strength?** Vs a player who pots his strong hands, 99 has no showdown value facing a pot bet, but might facing a smaller one. [05-12 teaches the same three steps.]
- **Showdown value's only goal is to REACH showdown [05-12].** It isn't about protection, charging draws, or dodging a tough river. **No "monkey betting":** in position, check back the turn (88 on J92, BTN vs BB). Out of position after the flop checks through, check-call a stab (44, BB vs BTN). Don't lead.

**Hand classes [08-18, 06-30]:** can play for stacks > thick value > thin value > draws > showdown value > air. (06-30 lists showdown value before draws. That's a ranging order, not a conflict.) "Thick value" depends on the villain: raise or donk it vs an under-bluffer, check-call it vs someone who bluffs enough or over-bluffs. Buckets depend on context: TPNK can play for stacks at 80bb vs a drunk whale, and bottom set may not at 400bb vs an "old man coffee warmer" [06-30].

## 2. Bet sizing: small or big, never in between
- **Small** keeps weak hands calling. Then you can bluff them on the river or get thin value, and you induce raises from strong hands.
- **Big** targets specific hands that won't fold, or folds out the whole range.
- **In-between** is the worst of both: better hands call and your targets fold. Two-thirds pot everywhere "doesn't make the most money" [08-25]. Todd's review flagged in-between sizes as one of his four leaks [09-15]. Miguel's 475 turn was "the dreaded in-between size" [08-04].
- **Size by the two questions [08-04]:**
  - **Uncapped:** go small, with value AND bluffs, or just call. Big raises only fold his junk and get snapped off. A small bet into two players works like a big bet heads-up.
  - **Capped + inelastic:** bet huge, pot to 1.6x. Multiway turn vs inelastic callers: pot to 1.2x, not 475.
  - **Capped + elastic:** go small for value to induce, or use a just-big-enough bluff size.
- **Sizing method:** name the hand you're targeting and ask, "What would I bet to bluff that hand off?" Value-bet a bit under that [08-25]. Gethen uses the same method [04-28]: AKss on a flush river bets 350-400 because 450-500 would fold Jx.
- **Round river bluffs UP [04-28].** Size for the hand you want to fold, then leave margin: "10% too small is much more costly than 10% too big." So a bluff can go BIGGER than your value size vs the same player (vs a trappy player: value 80-100, bluff 135-150).
- **Don't try to balance vs 2/5 unknowns [04-28].** Value, bluffs and showdown value each get their own line. Flop and turn sizes can match when value and bluffs want the same thing (build the pot), but the river sizes split. "The best bluff": he puts money in, then folds. (Same idea as 06-09's "gets called twice, then folds," deep-stacks section 6.)
- **Turn after a small flop bet [04-28]:** he's capped and his hands are inelastic (top pair, draws): bet big (150 after a 25 flop bet at 2/5, a pot-ish size). He's uncapped (flushes still in his range) or trappy: bet small again so his flushes raise and his weak hands put in more money before folding the river. A big turn bet "just narrows their range."
- **Size for the hands that call [08-11]:**
  - KK on a K-high board: go small on the turn. His range is mostly weak, and his traps raise. A giant bet "to reduce SPR" is the mistake.
  - Nut flush on the turn: no need to go huge. His flushes check-raise a small bet anyway.
  - Vs a fish, don't match value and bluff sizes "to be balanced." He can't exploit you.
- **Don't shrink a bluff you believe folds most of his range [08-11].** Running into trip 4s sometimes is fine.

**IP flop and turn defaults [undated HHP: start-over]** (live cash, about 2/5, rec BB defender; HHP calls these examples that "save processing power", not a rule everyone must use, and says HHP teaches defaults for almost every flop and turn in its boot camps, which this video does not cover):
- **Flop question:** "What size will he raise strong stuff and call weak stuff with?" Bet that size. Wet, dynamic flop (98dd: many draws, the nuts can change on the turn): about half pot in a single-raised pot ($25-30), about a third pot in 3-bet pots as a starting point. Strong hands (smaller straights, sets, two pair) raise and weak hands call.
- **No such size exists (static, dry, A-7-2):** "big bet or check", the rare case. 77/22 trap even a small bet, so no size gets strong hands to raise and weak hands to call, and any Ace "is not folding even for a large size." Go large (overbet) to pile money in against AQ, AJ, AT, A9, A8, and accept the occasional monster ("monsters under the bed"). He expects to check back "a lot" of turns here. See the ♣ #2 context list.)
- **Turn questions, in order:** (1) **Is he capped?** If he didn't raise the wet flop, he is mostly single pair. Yes = go big: his 78 or A7 and his Ace-high and King-high flush draws still don't fold, but he won't put in more money for a small size. (2) **Not capped:** can I pick a size where he raises strong and calls weak? Yes = **small** (turn flush card: small flushes put in more money "not wanting to face a bad run out" and 99 with the 9s keeps calling). No = big bet or check (A-7-2 after a big flop bet: 77/22/A2 "feel invulnerable", Ax still calls, so bet big again for the weaker Ax).
- **River, "bluff wants to go small, so value wants to go big":** Q-7-6 flush-draw board, flop half pot, uncapped turn $35, river bluff $75-100 (folds red 88 or QJ with no spade), river value $225-250 (flushes with a spade). Deviating has a price: a bigger flop bet lets him trap strong hands while weak hands go call, call, fold; checking back loses fold equity because he under-bluffs across turns and rivers.
- **Ace-high flush draw facing a flop raise (J-9-x, an aggressive rec):** his raise is mostly two pair (J9) and sets (22, 99), with an occasional combo draw. Don't 3-bet (no fold equity, you're behind sets and two pair), and don't fold an ace-high flush draw in position deep: call. On the nut-flush turn go large ($300) to set a river SPR under 1, and jam the river for value (he calls with J9, 22, 99; never bluff a river here).
- **Paired turn, uncapped (QTcc, J-9-2 flop he raised and we called, deuce turn, he checks):** boats and quads are now in his range, but a big turn bet leaves ONLY boats and quads, so go small: his combo draws and ace-high flush draws keep calling and you risk little vs a boat. A small river bluff then gets a good price to fold the Ace-high, King-high and Queen-high spade hands that beat or chop you. - **The inverse question, with Marc's four size patterns [2025-09-30].** With value ask "what size would my BLUFF need to make him fold?" With a bluff ask "what size would my VALUE bet need to get called?" Then size away from that. Vs live players value and bluff sizes are almost never the same, and it is NOT always "big bluffs, small value." The four patterns (2/5, $1K-1.5K effective unless noted):
  1. **Value small, bluff big.** He's capped and weak on the river (his sets and flushes already raised earlier). Value about 150, "3/4 pot" (the pot is under $200), so Tx calls. Bluff 220-285, "a little sauce on top," so Tx folds. ([2025-04-08 HHP] same numbers: river value about 150 so Qx calls, bluff 225-240 as a small overbet so Qx folds.) Vs a trappy player be careful with the bluff size ("we can run into brick walls"). Vs a fearful fast-player go a bit bigger still.
  2. **Value jam, bluff tiny.** His range splits into monsters and junk (we 3-bet KK, the K turns, the river is an A, his two pair won't fold). Value jams. The bluff only needs to fold TT-QQ/9x, so bet 125-150 ("breathe on this pot"). Turn was small (80) because he's capped and elastic.
  3. **Value tiny, bluff medium.** Thin value vs a live BB with Jx: a quarter to a third of pot (100-125) so Jx calls, and fold to a check-raise (live players don't raise thin or turn hands into bluffs). Bluff about half pot (200-250) to fold Jx plus the bricked draws. A tiny bluff gets called by Jx and a big one runs into two pair. Sets jam. Marc says the solver would almost never use 1/4-1/3 pot, but live players don't exploit it.
  4. **Value about pot, bluff at an extreme.** He's capped with no two pair+ (Qx and missed draws). Value takes the biggest size QJ/QT never fold (about pot, 400-500). Bluff: jam vs a scared player who folds Qx, tiny vs a sticky player or an unknown ("we're going to get snapped by the queen X, but the price is good"). Which bluff makes more is "quite player dependent": watch showdowns.
- **Capped but inelastic, size UP for value, even thin [2025-12-23].** Marc's read of the low-stakes pool: it calls more than it used to.
  - **Second pair, paired river, he checks:** K10 in a BTN vs BB pot. We checked back the turn, the river pairs, he checks. Overbet 1.5-2x pot. Small, medium or large all get called by 6x/77/88, and Marc figures K10 is ahead "90 plus%."
  - **Q-high 3-bet pot, top pair:** small flop c-bet, turn 250 ("could even argue for 300") to set the SPR, jam the river (bottom-card-pairing rivers make Qx/JJ/TT "more sticky").   - **4-straight or 4-flush river:** go "egregiously large," "maybe even with 55." Callers on those boards hold much more than straights (ATcc, two pair, even 5x). Value wins and big bluffs don't. (Vs players who DO fold here, size down with value and up with bluffs.)
  - **3-bet pot, we hold QQ, the river pairs the board:** overbet all-in. Tx/JJ call "even for $1,100." Don't shut down out of fear of trips.
  - **Static dry A-high board, we hold top pair (AT) in the BB, the BTN checked back the flop:** overbet the turn and the river. His checked-back Ax won't fold even for a large size, and a $15-30 turn bet is a "massive mistake." *(⚖ vs check-and-check-raise in section 5.)*
  - **A rec bets big flop, big turn, then checks the river:** his range is value-heavy (AK/KQ/AA/KJ). Jam your value. One big bet won't fold him: "tell him twice."
  - **Low SPR, about 125bb effective (J92, we overbet the turn):** don't jam the river. Top pair doesn't fold at that depth, and it takes 2-3x pot river bets to make it think. - **In position as the 3-bettor: the full system [2025-10-21, *You're STILL Playing 3-Bet Pots Wrong*, Marc; his on-screen charts were checked against the spoken hands].** 2/5, about $900 effective (~180bb) unless noted.
  - **Flop:** c-bet your WHOLE range small, about 1/3 to 40% pot (in an SRP it's half to 2/3). The absolute pot is bigger, so the money is "scarier." Pick the size where he fast-plays strong hands and calls with weak ones. Vs a big bet ($90-110) he traps strong hands "at a much higher frequency."
  - **Static, dry A-high board:** in an SRP Marc plays big-bet-or-check (his sets 77/22 won't fast-play). In a 3-bet pot he range-bets small, then keeps going: he has fewer top pairs and more pocket pairs that fold later, his Ax folds by the river because he puts you on AK, and at low SPR bet-bet-bet into a trapped set costs little. (♣ #2 context below.)
  - **Turn, decided by his range** (chart: capped or uncapped, then fast-play or inelastic):
    - **Uncapped and slow-plays** (traps 99, 22, A9, A2): value bets BIG (put the money in for him). Showdown value (KK, QQ) checks. Bluffs check the flop and turn (the chart), then bluff the river if he checks it, because a river check caps him. If he bets the river, fold.
    - **Uncapped and fast-plays** (a flush card, he check-raises 8-high/Q-high flushes): value AND bluffs bet SMALL (weak hands call, strong hands raise, "win-win"), showdown value (TT) checks, then fire the river with the bluff if called.
    - **Capped and inelastic** (a wet flop where Q9/99/66 would have raised, he has Qx, JT, A-high draws): value bets BIG (about 280 in his example). Showdown value (JJ, 9x) checks back, "my favorite line in poker," and bluff-catches a smaller river bet. Bluffs bet big but only the biggest size that still leaves a "big chunky overbet" on the river (about 180 in his example).
    - **Capped and elastic** (KK after a turn K, he folds QQ/A9 to a big bet): value AND bluffs bet SMALL so 9x/TT/JJ come to the river, then split. He's player-dependent: an aggressive player fast-plays 44/22 (so he's capped), a super-passive one traps all his sets (uncapped). "It's about asking good questions," not a flowchart.
  - **River** (chart, four branches):
    - **Uncapped and fast-plays** (flush river, he checks): value SMALL (Qx calls, flushes raise), bluff MEDIUM (450-500 in his example, folds Qx/JJ/TT/99/8x), showdown value small on the flop and checks turn and river.
    - **Uncapped and does NOT fast-play** (Nitty Ned, who never raises a flush): value BIG, showdown value checks, bluffs small on the river.
    - **Capped and inelastic** ($847 pot, $580 behind): value JAMS (Qx won't fold), bluffs SMALL (250-280, folds missed spade draws).
    - **Capped but deep** (about 2x pot behind, elastic): value takes the biggest size AQ/AJ/AT still call (about pot, $800-900), bluff JAMS. Test: you wouldn't jam AK for value because you think they'd fold, so jam your bluffs. "Small is just a starting point," not $200-300.
- **Plan the river before you size the turn [04-14, 01-27].** Size the turn DOWN to carry TT/JJ/A9s (01-27) or to keep a river overbet behind (04-14). If the river improves the hand you wanted to fold (the jam's target AQ now has a queen), size down to a bet that still folds AJ/A9s/A5s [04-14].
- **No autopilot 1/3, 2/3, 3/4 [01-27].** "They only have two buttons." KK on a wet, dynamic board: bigger every street (open 25 not 20, flop 25-30 not 15, turn 150 not 50, river 250-300). Turned nut flush: 25-35 so small flushes raise, or big. Never the in-between 60, which folds 9x and gets fewer raises.
- **Multiway with the second nuts: raise the flop 6-8x, not 4x [04-14]** ("egregious"). Then size UP on the turn, not 2/3-3/4 ("garbage"). An EP open, a multiway c-bet and a next-to-act call mean someone loves his hand.
- **Vs a passive CALLER, "go for gold" [02-17].** He never puts money in for you, so size it in yourself: river 2.5x pot with the nuts, no "fancy" small sizes. Paired turn with a full house: the biggest size his flush draws still call ("maybe pot"), then 2.5x on the river vs Kx. Vs a slightly aggressive player, go small on that turn so trips raise.
- **Vs an aggro CALLER [02-17]:** nuts on the turn = 2-2.5x pot (his hands are inelastic). River "spaz bait": a tiny bet when his range is trips or busted draws. Marc "almost never" does this vs passive players.
- **Don't go small again on a blank turn when he's already capped [08-11].** That just walks him to the river cheaply. *(09-15 went small-small to funnel an OMC on a queen turn. - **In small pots, recs react to the dollar amount, not the % of pot** [09-15]. $100 into $60 still gets called by A7.
- **Don't size down out of fear of the few combos that beat you.** "If we run into a brick wall occasionally, it's okay" [09-15, 08-11].
- **Examples [08-25]:**
  - Turn barrel when an overcard hits a paired flop: size down so 44 and 76 keep calling for the river bluff.
  - River flush: go bigger to target trips, or much smaller to target 99-77. Pick one.
  - Tight player's turn: go small (35-45) to keep small pairs in.
- **Iso pots** are closer to a 3-bet pot. C-bet a bit under half pot [08-25].
- **Paired board, uncapped villain: small turn, small river [06-30].** Draws get "more elastic" and stay in, nutted hands may donk, and a tiny river bluff folds A-high flush draws cheaply. Compare: a capped range on an unpaired flush turn means go huge.

- **Small IP river bets work live [06-23].** The solver avoids them because of check-raises, and live players rarely check-raise after a triple barrel. Bet under half pot to fold the busted draws that beat you.

**The "medium size" lesson [2025-07-15, *Most Players Use This Bet Size... It's KILLING Your Win Rate*, Marc, 2/5].** His student Alex was at about $35/hr and, banned from any 1/3-to-pot size on turns and rivers, doubled his win rate in a month (Marc's numbers). The size to avoid is the middle one (about 1/3 to pot) on turns, rivers and raises. His spots:
- **Raising a flop stab.** Vs a BIG stab on a wet board (a strong range: he bets big with good hands "70-80%" of the time), check-raise with value HUGE: 6x ($300 over $50), not the common 3-4x. A medium raise "only lights money on fire." Don't bluff that range. Vs a SMALL stab ("monkey stabbing"), still go big (250-275, not 75-100): weak hands fold to any raise and the hands that continue are inelastic and call 75 or 275 alike. You'll play fewer medium pots and more big ones. Marc's solver screen has top pair+ bet big only about 21% at equilibrium; live players are unbalanced.
- **Turn, capped but inelastic check-caller (blank turn):** bet big, value or bluff ($125-150, not 2/3-3/4 pot). The same hands that call 75 call 125-150, and a big bluff builds a bigger river pot ($800 behind in a $400 pot folds Qx). Make sure he's really capped.
- **Turn that changes the nuts or pairs the board:** bet SMALL (a full house: small so 9x raises and draws stay in; draws get more elastic when the board pairs).
- **River:** vs a super-capped range (he raised his strong hands earlier, so small flop, small turn, then) bluff 1.5-2x pot, since 90-95% of his range folds. Vs a split range (weak stuff plus two pair, QQ on an ace river) value goes ALL-IN, and you'd hate to bluff big into AT/A5/A4. Thin value goes small (about 1/3 pot).
- **He check-raised the flop, then checks a flush turn:** two pair and sets, few flushes (draws are only "10-15%" of his check-raises). Bet the flush "egregiously" big: about 400, about 300 vs a nit, more vs a sticky player, the biggest size two pair/sets call.

**The "will he fast-play?" sizing system [2025-07-01 HHP, *You're Sizing Bets WRONG. Here's the Simple Fix.*, 2/5, about 200bb].** Deliberately unbalanced; the video says live players won't exploit it. Two questions: (1) will he fast-play strong hands on this board? (2) is he capped or uncapped?
- **Flop, he WILL fast-play (wet, dynamic board: the nuts can change):** value AND bluffs use the same size, the biggest where he raises strong and calls weak. About half pot heads-up in an SRP, about 1/3 pot or a bit smaller multiway ($70 into 200 with five players; the next-to-act player raises less), about 1/3 in a 3-bet pot. Live players react to the absolute dollar size, and a small size lets weak hands (66, A-high) hold across turns so bluffs get better.
- **Flop, he will NOT fast-play (dry static board like A72: only a wheel gutshot, the nuts change on almost nothing):** he doesn't raise strong hands and doesn't call weak ones (33, KJ). Split the range. Value goes BIG (overbet, 60-70 in his example). Bluffs mostly check back and wait for turn information. Don't c-bet the whole range. His rule of thumb for "big": the biggest size the bottom 10% of the top of his range calls (A3-A6s on A72).
- **Turn, he will fast-play (flush and straight cards):** the same size for value and bluffs, about 30-40% pot in an SRP and 25-30% in a 3-bet pot. A bluff this small gets his flushes to raise (you fold cheaply) and carries his weak hands to the river.
- **Turn, he won't fast-play and is CAPPED (a blank after he raised his strong hands on the flop):** value AND bluffs big, the biggest size a weak Tx calls (120-150 in his example).
- **Turn, he won't fast-play and is UNCAPPED (an ace turn in a 3-bet pot, K-K-x board):** value big (no overbet needed at low SPR), bluffs check, and bluff the river only if he checks.
- **River, four shapes** (the first two are about 90% of hands): (a) capped range: bluff big, value small (target Jx). (b) A range of two pair+ vs junk: bluff small, value big or all-in. (c) Value medium (KK, about 450-550, because AJ folds to a 2x-pot jam 80-85% of the time at 2/5-5/10), bluff tiny or all-in. (d) Bluff medium (250-300), value tiny (about 100-150) or huge. The all-in bottom set goes when his range has lots of strong two pair. Size down if he folds one-pair ace hands to a jam, if you hold a blocker, or if he 3-bets AJ preflop (few two pair).

  - **3-bet pot IP:** c-bet the whole range small (about $60) and check back marginal turns (his AK float can be a bluff), then call the river bet. "My favorite line in live poker."
  - **River, quarter-pot bet IP** vs a caller who won't fold a K but folds ace-high/J-high flush draws and QJ: "not a thing in equilibrium," but it folds them.

- **In position, heads-up, as the raiser: the three-question flowchart [2025-02-04 HHP, *Ask THESE 3 Questions*].** The IP twin of the OOP flowchart in section 5. Ask: (1) capped or uncapped, (2) will he fast-play strong hands, (3) what is my hand (value, showdown value, bluff)?
  - **Uncapped and he WILL fast-play (wet or dynamic board, J-9-2):** value, showdown value AND bluffs all bet small. A small bluff makes him cap himself when he only calls. TT bets small, checks the turn, and snap-calls a river bet of half pot or less (his range is A-high and busted draws). "Static vs dynamic matters more than wet vs dry": on 9-4-2 many turns change the nuts so he fast-plays, on A-2 the ace is the nuts on almost every turn so he doesn't.
  - **Uncapped and he will NOT fast-play (A-7-2, or a paired board where an ace can come):** value bets BIG (AK: 60 into 42), showdown value (QQ) and bluffs check back so he caps himself, then bluff big when he checks the river. Small-then-large bluff barrels "do not work" here because a flop call leaves his range undefined. (Sits on the "don't range-bet A72" side of ♣ #2.)
  - **Capped:** does he have inelastic hands (weak Ax, Qx that never fold on the turn)? Yes: value AND bluffs bet big on the turn ("the best bluff is one where they call the turn and fold the river"); the river then goes value big, bluff small if he is still inelastic, or value small (the biggest size his top pair calls, $150) and bluff big (2x-2.5x pot) if he is elastic. No inelastic hands: both small on the turn.
  - **Uncapped flush turn (he plays draws passively):** bluff and value both small on flop and turn so his flushes raise and weak hands stay, then a big river bluff ($260 with 87dd; value $120 with AQss, the biggest size QJ/KJ call).
  - **Elasticity is player dependent:** most players in 2025 won't stack off with top pair on the river (elastic: bluff 2x pot, value just under pot, $300 with 99). A fish who never folds top pair is inelastic (value 2.5x pot, bluff small to fold ace-high and king-high draws). Live, absolute dollars matter more than the pot fraction.
  - **3-bet pot:** overpairs at SPR under 1 are inelastic ("I can't overbet"): value jams, bluffs go tiny (T9cc folds the ace-high and king-high draws). KK on a dry-but-dynamic 9-4-2 with a K turn: small, small, small ($130 river so TT-QQ hero-call); the bluff conflict is in the ⚖ table below. K-K-4 with an ace-of-the-flush-suit turn (he "won't fast-play" a flush, he's "terrified of AK"): value small flop, big turn, big river; a bluff (T9dd) checks the turn and bets 300 on a river check. Board differs from [06-30]'s paired-flop small-small, and his flushes lead the river here.
  - **Monotone (Q-8-4 spades, a fourth spade on the turn, he checks):** the bluff (JT) checks the turn back, then bets small ($50-60, pot $82) if he checks the river too, because some hands (KsXs) call anything. With the second nuts (KsQd) go big.
- **River range in buckets [2024-11-26 HHP, *My Bankroll Exploded*, 2/5, river jam 775; agrees with the four patterns in [2025-09-30] and [2025-07-01 HHP]].** Bucket his river range (weak, strong, missed draws; a bucket counts only above about 10%):
  - **Mostly strong + missed draws (a player who 3-bet-called and plays flush draws passively):** value BIG (the 775 jam), bluff SMALL (150, about 30% pot: folds AK/AQ with a diamond and sometimes 77/99). Humans call too much with a strong range instead of folding some of it.
  - **Mostly weak + missed draws (he limp-called or iso-called wide):** value SMALL relative to the jam (250-350, "the biggest size where Tx/8x still calls"; small is relative to your all-in), bluff BIG (jam), because a weak range can't take the heat and humans fold too much.
  - **Strong + weak + missed draws:** value very BIG or very SMALL (a jam with thin value like AQ of diamonds folds the JJ/QQ that pay), bluff MEDIUM (300, about 2/3 pot: loses the minimum to his top, folds QQ/JJ/KTs/JTs/99 and the draws).
  - **Turn, he plays draws passively (uncapped):** small with bluffs AND value (100 into 262) so he raises flushes and calls with weak stuff. "Humans should almost never be balanced," and it is NOT "always overbet bluffs, small value": it depends on his range.
  - **[2024-12-31 HHP] same lesson:** QQ in a 3-bet pot, flop $50 small, blank turn BIG to max value (a small turn brings no extra money), river 400-600 rather than a jam because the pool folds Tx to a jam. The jam is the BLUFF size (KQs, ace-high: "80, 85, 90%" fold). Agrees with [2025-12-16] (AA value 800-1,000, the all-in is the bluff).
- **Fold equity rule [2024-10-01 HHP, *Here's Why Your Bluffs Aren't Working*].** The more fold equity, the less hand equity you need. Fold equity is high only when he is capped AND you are deep. If either is half true, blasting air "starts to become a punt." First question on the flop: will he raise strong and call weak? Yes: bet small a lot. No: big bet or check.
  - **Capped + deep (Q-9-7 two-tone, small flop bet, blank turn):** big turn with ATs AND 54dd (he won't fold Qx or JT anyway), jam the river 2.5x pot (AQ/KQ fold). With KK you wouldn't jam to target KQ, since KQ folds, "which shows the bluff is where the fold equity is."
  - **Flush turn (uncapped):** small with everything so he raises flushes, then a big river once he is capped (his best hand is AQ). With ATs the coach says the value size on a completed flush board is small (his figure: about 125-150), so the 54dd bluff can use the big size with little fear of a brick wall.
  - **In-between J turn (open-enders and two pair possible; he's only "sort of capped"):** high-equity hands (ATs) go big, pure air (54dd) goes small to re-cap him and make him show his straights. Thick value and high-equity bluffs take the biggest size, low-equity bluffs and thin value the smaller one.
  - **Shallow:** small overbet with the nuts, check back bricks, tiny river bets only to fold his draws. Top pair won't fold shallow.
  - **Dry static A-9-7 (he won't raise strong or call weak):** big bet or check. Bluff only with the highest equity (T8dd) or 7x blockers that block his traps (76dd, 75ss), and deep, overbet-bet-jam (225bb+). Uncapped and shallow is "maybe the worst spot to bluff."
- **Turn and river sizes vs a rec [2024-12-10 HHP, *The 9 Biggest Mistakes*, 1/2-2/5].**

**Where a SMALL bet makes more than a big one [undated HHP, *The Bet Sizing Mistake That's Costing You BIG*; live cash, 2/5-style SRP and 3-bet pots, example hands at 5/5 and 5/10 and 400bb+ deep in one; the speaker isn't named on the captions].** The rule in the video: "the bigger the absolute size of the pot, the smaller our bet has to be," and "elastic simply means price sensitive." If it checks through, he is capped: river bluff about 200-225 to fold KK/QQ/JJ ("don't need to go particularly big"), and with Aces bet "very very small" to string him along for the last street from KK-JJ. Vs a very passive villain who may check back AQ/AK, or an aggressive one with A4s/A3s/A5s, go BIGGER on the river to target the Ax that checked back. "Just going bet bet bet is not going to get in all the money even in a four-bet pot" at that depth. *(Same flop and turn plan as the 4-bet pot entry in section 2 below; the river numbers differ, see the ⚖ CONFLICT table.)*

## 3. Value-bet thinner and bigger
Todd's #1 leak (an HHP student, not Joan): too conservative with value [09-15].
- **Top pair multiway, checked to you:** bet (30-40, not $10). Q-T, Jx and 77-99 won't bet, but won't fold either [09-15].
- **A set vs a sticky, passive rec:** size up every street and bet the river big [09-15].
- **Low SPR, strong hand, passive calling station:** bet the flop and jam the turn. "He's not going to make a betting mistake" [09-15].
- **A queen on a paired river after you overbet the turn:** bet $350-400, "maybe even $500." The pool calls a chunky bet with a queen, especially when the board pairs [08-11].
- **A rec who double-barreled, then checks the river:** he's check-calling a strong range. With trips, bet bigger than 2/3 pot (about 450-500) [08-11].

**Current answer, trips vs a rec who bets the flop, bets big on the turn, then checks the river [08-11].** Bet bigger than 2/3 pot (about 450-500 with A8 trips), not all-in. *Superseded by 08-11:* [2025-08-19] "consider jamming" and [2025-12-23] "jam value." The read (his bet-bet-check is check-call strength) is the same in all three.
- **The same line vs a PRO [2025-08-19]:** a pro's bet-bet-check is usually a give-up (pros value-bet thin), so with trips bet "egregiously small, insulting" to induce a spaz. The same check means opposite things: the rec's is strength planning to call, the pro's is mostly a give-up. Don't bluff the rec's river 250-300: either give up, or bet tiny ($75-80) to fold an A-high flush draw. One river bet only "tells him once."
- **Vs a passive player who bets half pot and gives up rivers: DONK-LEAD your value BIG [2025-05-20 HHP, 2025-04-22 HHP].** Checking misses no bluffs (he doesn't fire them), and his bet would be a smaller size than yours. Two pair on a blank river vs a half-pot bettor: lead 240, which top pair calls and which avoids a check-raise that "sets off alarm bells" (he can fold top pair to one). Same idea vs a player who checked back 8-high on the river (an under-bluffer): donk your river straight large, since he only bets value and Kx calls a lead. With ONE pair vs that passive bettor: check, fold to a pot bet, check-raise all-in vs a half-pot bet (he folds AK/AQ/AJ), and if he checks back you win.
- **He checks a flush-completing river he'd donk with a flush [2025-04-22 HHP, 2025-04-08 HHP].** He has no flush, so bet thin value (Qx, two pair) with impunity after your turn overbet, and use big overbet bluffs with bricked draws (T9cc all-in folds AJ/QJ/KJ). Vs a player who does NOT donk flushes, don't jam a bluff: at most a small one [2025-05-06 HHP].
- **Thin value when he checks a nut-changing river [2024-11-12 HHP, *Why Bluffing Is SO GOOD*, 3-bet pot BTN vs CO, about 200bb].** He raised his strong hands on the flop and turn and donks his flushes on the river (a rec "often donks" strong hands on nut-changing rivers), so his check is crippled: value bets small (125 into about 400, even with the nut flush; JJ/TT/9x call). Small turn sizes make him donk the river more, and larger turn sizes make him donk less (he thinks you'll bet thin); a blank river makes him trap more. On nut-changing turns both thin value and low-equity bluffs size down. River donks and how to answer them: `playbook-postflop-bluffs-and-rivers.md`.

- **Top pair with a clear best hand vs a pro: put in more money [2024-12-10 HHP].** Flatting AKo early vs early to let a fish in is fine, but with top pair top kicker raise the turn or bet the river for thin value. Checking the river back was the mistake ("we just have the best hand 90% of the time"). If you trap AA, the river is a mandatory value bet. A KQo 3-bettor at SPR about 1 vs a rec who gets sticky with 99/TT/JJ: jam the river (agrees with the Q-high 3-bet pot line [2025-12-23]).
- **Capped + inelastic multiway after the flop checks through:** lead the turn big with top pair. Checking "missed quite a bit of value" [08-04].
- **Capped + elastic villain checks the river:** bet really small to induce. Checking back was "a huge missed value" [08-04].
- **A 4-way 3-bet pot vs suited broadways and pairs:** c-bet, and at SPR about 1 on the turn, put it in [09-15].
- **Gethen's thin-value test [04-28]:** bet if worse hands call more than 50% of the time. "If I value own myself, so be it."
  - KK at 300bb after calling his flop check-raise, with turn and river checked to you: check back the turn (too thin for two streets), then bet 250-300 on the river. 350-400 would fold AJ.
  - KK in a 4-bet pot on an A-high board: river 125, the same as AA's size. "If you're not value betting thin and sometimes value-owning yourself, you're probably leaving money on the table."
- **Fast-play sets vs a passive caller [02-17]:** a small flop check-raise ("just click this") sets up a turn jam. Giving rope gets you nothing vs a player who won't bet thin or bluff.
- **Vs a player who calls down with every pair: no bluffs, thin value [02-10].**
- **Sticky villain with draws and weak aces: overbet the turn [04-14]** (1.5x pot with top pair). The value is on the turn, not the river.
- **A rec leads the turn, then checks the river: value-bet [04-14].** He's check-calling 8x, 7x, 66, 99. Judge a bet by its DOLLAR amount for the game, not just its % of pot.
- **Thin value in a multiway pot when he checks a scary river [04-14]:** bet small (QQ, a one-liner river). "Don't be intimidated into checking back."
- **Two pair vs a sticky, aggressive "non-believer" betting 80% on the river: value-raise [04-14].** Just calling is the easy miss.
- **Your draw comes in, and his range is weak pairs (77-QQ): size DOWN on the river [04-14].** Overbet the turn to pressure the pairs, then a smaller river. Size off his range, not your hand.
- **Middle set vs a passive fish, board pairs his trips on the river [04-28]:** jam the full house. "Put the money in for them." A tiny induce bet only vs an ego player.
- **Thin value vs a live BB with Jx: bet a quarter to a third of pot and fold to a check-raise [2025-09-30].** The size patterns are in section 2 (the inverse question, pattern 3). Live players don't raise thin, so a small bet isn't exploited.
- **You make the flush and his check-back range over-calls: jam for value [2025-12-16].** Multiway, he checked the turn back, so his range is 88-TT, A7, 76 and Qx of clubs, and the pool calls those MORE than equilibrium. Overbet-jam about 2.5-3x pot. At equilibrium the solver prefers a smaller size, but once he over-calls the jam becomes the highest-EV play and the all-in BLUFF the lowest. The point is whether he calls or folds more than equilibrium, not an exact number.
